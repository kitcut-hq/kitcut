# Is there an oil well under your house? Los Angeles

The one thing the film says first: the state's own records put about twenty-four thousand oil and gas wells under Los Angeles County, and one old field west of downtown sits under homes, most of its wells still listed as idle, not plugged.

## Unconfirmed

- How many wells sit directly under a house today: the state's points are not surveyed to the house, and the city says buried wells' mapped spots are "sometimes only approximate". The film shows where the records put them and never says a given house has a well.
- Undocumented wells that are on no record at all.

## Commonly misreported

- The 1985 Ross Dress-for-Less methane explosion is often blamed on an old well; the city's task force found the more probable source was not an oil well. Leave it out.
- Porter Ranch (2015) was a gas-storage leak, not an old oil well. Leave it out.
- "Doheny struck oil in 1892": the state's sheet says the field was discovered in 1893.
- City counts (5,130 wells in the City of LA, 2018) are not county counts. The film uses the county count from WellSTAR.

## The film must not say

- "under your house" as a fact about any viewer's home (only as the question)
- "leaking" about any well except the two Echo Park wells of 2016
- "explosion"
- "dangerous" or "toxic"
- "banned" without "phase out within twenty years"
