# Custom cartoons as a done-for-you service, researched 2026-10-23

The question: should KitCut sell **made-for-you animated films**? Two buyers:

- **Families**: an episode starring their child, pet or whole family, made from their photos and
  real stories (the house, the car, the daycare, the catchphrases). We made a series like this
  for the owner's own family, and the Duchess cat series, and both came out well.
- **Companies**: a custom animated explainer, product film, brand story, training film or event
  promo, made by us with our own tool.

**How the numbers were measured.** Google Keyword Planner through the Builders HQ market-data
server (Google Ads API), English, Google Search only, average monthly searches over Oct 2025 to
Sep 2026, plus the planner's top-of-page bid range (what advertisers pay for the top slot, our
best stand-in for what a click is worth). "—" means the planner had no number (under ~10 a
month). The planner folds close variants together: "2d animation studios" and "2d animation
companies" are one count, so rows were de-duplicated before anything was summed. Forecasts are
the planner's own, phrase match, nothing bought.

offers. What the offer has to prove: a portfolio per use case, the client's real logo, screens
and people, a free 10–15 s sample on their own product, fixed revision rounds.

## 5. Use cases: a market to create, one ad each

and companies may still want it once they see one. So the test is many ads, one use case each,
each showing a finished example film, and the cost of a serious enquiry ranks them. The signal
column is the nearest measured search (US a month, top bid): it shows the money and the
occasion around a use case, not demand for our film. Price bands are what to test, anchored on
section 3 and 4 prices, not proven.

## 6. Conclusions, and a test that would settle it

**What is proven.**
1. Families spend on their own story and their children's: personalised books (Wonderbly 10M
   sold, Hooray Heroes €27M a year), StoryWorth and Remento (~60,000 brand searches a month),
   pet portraits, Santa videos. Mostly at $25–100, and piling up in November and December.
2. Well-off families pay **$3k–50k** for bespoke keepsakes about their own family: a child's
   painted portrait ($4k–9.5k), a ghost-written memoir ($3k–30k), a legacy film ($1.4k–80k).
3. Companies pay **$2.4k–8k** (median quote $5,400) for a custom 60–90 s animated video from an
   agency, and $10k–75k from a premium studio, with 2–16 weeks' wait.
4. Personal products for children and pets sell from cold Meta ads.

**What is not proven.**
1. That anyone pays $2k–15k for an *animated* family film or series. Nobody sells one: one tiny
   studio (StoryOf) sells bespoke animated family films at a price on request, and Neiman Marcus
   offered a Disney short starring the buyer for $510,000 with no confirmed sale.
2. That a cold Meta ad can sell a $2k+ personal film. The local legacy-film studios advertising
   there all sell a consultation, not the film.
3. Search demand for the product itself: under ~200 searches a month in the US, every phrase.

**The cheap end is closed to us, and that is fine.** Personal animated films already sell on
Meta at $27–49 (Wondertoon, Custom Love Story) and on Etsy/Fiverr at $7–390, AI-made and
offshore. The premium end is empty. A fast studio that directs every film can own it, if the
offer is about the person behind it: a director's interview with the family, their real places
and in-jokes, a premiere screening, a printed book of stills. The price anchor is the child's
commissioned portrait: **a portrait that moves, talks and has its own jokes, ~$5k–12k a season,
in weeks instead of 6–12 months.** For companies the open space is the middle: an agency-grade
custom look in days for $500–2,400, and series sold as series.

**The test (proposed 2026-10-23, nothing bought).**
- **Six use cases, one ad set each, Meta, US.** F1 your child's series, F2 your pet's series, F4
  love story, F7 grandparent's life story, F14 bar/bat mitzvah film, F10 social stories. Each ad
  is 20–30 s of a finished example film, then a family's reaction, then one line and "From
  $X". (Wondertoon's and Custom Love Story's ads show what works: a free preview and reactions.)
- **The ad format (the owner's idea, 2026-10-23): a vertical split screen**, the cartoon on top
  and the person it is about watching it below: the child seeing his own episode, grandparents
  watching their life story, a couple their love story, an owner and pet watching the pet's.
  The reaction must be real and recorded by the family (a phone just below the screen, at eye
  level, the cartoon's sound audible so the two can be lined up by sound; the first viewing
  only). Open on the moment of recognition, then three or four gags each with its laugh, then
  the offer; 15 s and 30 s cuts. Post it unpaid on Instagram/TikTok first: comments asking "how
  do I get one" are free demand. A child's face in a paid ad: keep the school, home and street
  out of the chosen scenes.
- **The ask is a conversation, not a checkout:** a short form ("tell us about your family",
  photos optional) and a booked 20-minute call with the director. The price shows on the page.
- **Audience:** parents (Meta's parent segments), pet owners, newly engaged and anniversary life
  events where they still exist, narrowed to high-income ZIP codes; then lookalikes of anyone
  who books. B2B (C1 client holiday films) is a separate test: LinkedIn and our own contacts,
  not Meta, and only until early December.
- **Budget:** $25 a day per use case for 14 days, ~$2,100 in all. At the 2025 average cost per
  lead ($28–31) and a stricter form, expect ~15–40 leads in total.
- **Rules:** pause a use case after $300 with no lead that describes a real family; after $600
  with no booked call. Keep a use case that books a call under ~$200. The test succeeds if any
  use case produces one paid deposit, or three calls where the buyer accepts the price.
- **Christmas:** a series cannot be delivered by Dec 20, so sell the gift as a certificate with a
  personal teaser trailer delivered before Christmas and the premiere in January.

**Channels beyond ads, for the premium end** (how portrait and legacy sellers find buyers):
private-school auction lots (donate one season; the bids price it), bar/bat mitzvah planners,
estate planners who bundle legacy films, local screenings, word of mouth.
