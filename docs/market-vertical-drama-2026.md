# Vertical short drama (micro-drama, 短剧), researched 2026-10-14

What the industry looks like in 2025-2026, and a rulebook for one job: ten standalone, animated,
~60-second 9:16 dramas (illustrated pictures animated in the browser, a TTS voice per character,
music and effects, burned-in captions). Each is a whole story, not episode 1 of 80.

**How to read the numbers.** Market figures disagree because they measure different things:
app-store purchases (Sensor Tower), all revenue including China (Omdia), revenue outside China
(Media Partners Asia, "MPA"). Most craft rules come from practitioner blogs, some selling AI
script tools. † means the article was blocked and read only through search summaries. Numbers
in brackets point to `## Sources`.

## 1. Market

**Size.**
- Global, all channels including China: about **$11bn in 2025, $14bn forecast for 2026**; China
  was 83% of 2025 (Omdia, Oct 2025, via [5][6]).
- Outside China: **$2.7bn in 2025, $3.6bn in 2026, $9.5bn by 2031** (MPA, Aug 2026 [3]).
- App-store purchases worldwide: **$750m in Q1 2026, +20% a year**; downloads **850m in Q1 2026,
  +140%** (Sensor Tower, Jun 2026 [2]). 2025 full year about $2.98bn, +115% (Sensor Tower,
  quoted second-hand, not opened).
- China: about $6.9bn in 2024, above its box office [12]; 2025 estimates run from 63bn yuan
  (DataEye) to ~100bn yuan with animated dramas (CNSA via CNBC† [40]). ByteDance's free app
  Hongguo had 356m monthly users in July 2026 (QuestMobile via [14]).

**Who leads outside China (2026 revenue share, MPA [3]).** ReelShort 29%, DramaBox 21%,
DramaWave 13%, NetShort 10%, GoodShort 6%, about 300 others 11%. ReelShort: $97m (2023) →
$400m (2024) → $785m (2025) → $1.05bn forecast for 2026 with its first profit (MPA via
Variety† [4]). Its marketing ate **55% of revenue in 2025** [3]. Southeast Asia, Latin America
and India take three quarters of downloads [2]; the US paid 49% of revenue in Q1 2025 [1].

**Who watches.**
- US: 66m monthly viewers in 2025, up from 26m, "primarily women aged 25-45" (Omdia via
  Reuters [5]); 16-24 21%, 25-34 24%, 35-44 22% (Fabric, US and Canada [6]).
- Getting younger and more male: men rose from **1.1% to 30.3%** of Holywater's My Drama monthly
  users in seven quarters. Ages 22-27 are now about a quarter of viewing. 70% watch in bed;
  viewing peaks Sunday 8-10 pm (Holywater/Owl & Co, Jul 2026; company data and a self-selected
  survey [8]).
- China: live-action viewers 61-68% female, core age 31-40; AI animated dramas 62% male, over
  60% aged 18-34 (DataEye via [13]).
- Time spent: 25 min a day per user worldwide, April 2026 [2]; ReelShort 35.7 min against
  Netflix's 24.8 on US phones, Q4 2025 (Omdia [5]).

**How they make money.**
- The **first 8-10 episodes are free**, then coins per episode or a weekly VIP pass of up to
  **$19.99 a week** [7]. In China the first paywall (卡一) sits at episodes 8-12 of 80, the second
  at 26-30, the third near 60 [17].
- Free, ad-supported apps (FreeReels, Melolo) lead downloads in emerging markets [2]. Ads are
  forecast at about 15% of ReelShort's revenue by 2028 [3].

**Funnels.**
- US apps' ad money, Jan-Sep 2025: Facebook 25%, TikTok 19%, Snapchat 16%, Instagram 8%, YouTube
  2% (Sensor Tower via eMarketer [6]). Holywater's men find shows through YouTube search [8].
- YouTube: microdramas passed **6.5bn views in H1 2026**, watch time +50% a year; "Shorts
  series" (seasons and episodes) shipped on 23 Sep 2026 [25].
- TikTok: a free stand-alone app, PineDrama, in the US and Brazil since Jan 2026 [26]; its series
  "Screen Time" drew 250m views in its first month [10].

## 3. Writing standards

**The opening.** The most detailed timing guide is a Chinese breakdown of 100+ hit episodes [16]:
- a hook in 0-3 s;
- core conflict, relationships and goal by 10 s, with backstory saved for later;
- the first reversal or payoff (爽点) **before 30 s**;
- an emotional beat **every 20-30 s**;
- the ending hook in the last 5-10 s, cut on the peak.

Its rule: "短剧/漫剧不存在慢开场" ("short and animated dramas have no slow openings"). Others
agree: the first 2 s (Japan's GOKKO, 12bn+ plays self-reported [31]); the proposition in 3 s and
the hook inside 6 s (TikTok's own guidance [23]); the first 5 s (InkTip [20]). Hook types [16]:
- **direct conflict**: a public confrontation, a betrayal, a slap;
- **suspense**: a gun, a mystery call, a document;
- **extreme contrast**: a fall from triumph to disgrace.

**Cadence.**
- The engine is suppression then release (压抑-爆发): humiliation, counter with evidence, new
  threat, more proof; two swings in about 20 s [16].
- A Western guide that watched 100+ series: a "jolt" every ~40 s and at least two emotional
  spikes per 90 s episode [19].
- "Twist Addiction" (shock reversals that shred logic) is a named failing [19]. A twist must
  surprise yet stay "within real logic"; the bigger the build-up, the bigger the payoff [39].

**Endings.** Each serial episode ends on a hook stronger than anything in it, of three kinds:
suspense (an identity or secret), crisis (an imminent threat) or reversal (a line that overturns
what came before) [16]. Plant the thread in the first 10 s; a new threat in the last seconds
"reads as a stunt, not a payoff"; end on an image, not a line [21].

**Dialogue and exposition.**
- Lines are "short, fragmented, sharp" (短、碎、锋利) [16]. Real Reel budgets about 500 script
  words per 90 s episode, action lines included [19].
- Explain the cause of a conflict later, or never [16]. Backstory in voice-over "turns drama into
  radio" [19].
- Chinese animated-drama writers keep dialogue under ~30% of the script and carry the rest with
  action, effects and short inner-voice lines (OS) with a clear emotion, using narration
  sparingly (practitioner post [38]†).

**Characters.** Each lead is a binary conflict you can say in four words ("rich bully vs.
underdog") plus one visual tell (a ring, a scar) [19]. Archetypes: the hidden billionaire or
heiress, the cold CEO, the humiliated wife or daughter-in-law, the scheming relative, the
alpha. On-screen name and status cards ("顾北辰 · 顾氏集团总裁") are common in Chinese dramas;
**no written source was found** for the convention.

Writers call the work "reverse-engineered around data-driven formulas" [22]; ReelShort's 20 US
writers turn out 15-20 scripts a month built on "tropes that sell" (Abby Dzeng [7]).

## 4. Genres and tropes

- **Romance is the core:** 74% of My Drama viewers watch it, 76% of women [8]. Sensor Tower lists
  romance, revenge, fantasy, family conflict and status reversal [2].
- **What was winning as 2026 began:** revenge, chaebol (rich-family) romance and power-imbalance
  stories; female-led revenge did especially well (SocialPeta, the week of 29 Dec 2025 [35]).
- **Classic Chinese staples:** rebirth-and-revenge, "Cinderella's counterattack", forced marriage
  turned love, soul-swaps, with a loud slap and a kneeling apology in every show [34]; plus
  campus romance, enemies-to-lovers and rags-to-riches [12]. Chinese AI animated dramas lean on
  cultivation fantasy (玄幻), overpowered comebacks and comedy (about 44% of supply) [13].
- **Growing:** men watch 1.4× more sci-fi and fantasy than women; horror and thriller are called
  underserved but growing [8]. AI makes dragon and mermaid fantasy affordable [12].
- **Tired:** in a 1,670-fan survey (mostly Western), the tropes that make people stop are
  slapping, banquet humiliation, violence against women, and drugged-sex or pregnancy plots.
  The 7.8% who now watch less blame repetitive plots, bullying, misogyny and violence. They want
  romcoms, fantasy, mystery and thrillers [11]†. Real Reel lists billionaire alphas, surprise
  pregnancies and werewolf mates as cliché overload [19].
- **The lead sells the show:** 88% say the lead actor decides what they watch [11]†.
- **West versus China.** Western hits keep the Chinese core (absolute power, protection,
  dramatic reversal) and wrap it in werewolves, vampires and elite families. Chinese scripts with
  Western faces felt wrong ("the face is right, the flavor is wrong"), so locals now rewrite them
  [36]. Western directors had to be persuaded that slaps are essential [37]†, and Western fans
  are the ones now tiring of them [11].

## 5. AI and animation

**China.**
- 430,000 micro-dramas went online in the first eight months of 2026, **over 90% made with AI**
  (NRTA via [14]).
- AI animated drama (AI漫剧, 动态漫) was worth 16.8bn yuan in 2025, forecast at 22-30bn for 2026
  [13][15].
- Four of the top five on Hongguo's chart were AI animated dramas in July 2026 [13].
- Cost per minute fell from 2,000-5,000 yuan (mid-2025) to under 1,000, some teams 400; a whole
  drama costs 50,000-240,000 yuan against 500,000-800,000 shot live [13]. Another report has the
  cheapest end rising again in 2026 as platforms raised the bar [14].
- Hit rate: 0.47% of 221,900 AI dramas passed 100m views in H1 2026 (DataEye [14]); under 1.3%
  earned back their cost (unattributed) [14].

**Outside China.**
- AI series cost $60-100k against $100-300k shot live (THR†, Sep 2026 [30]). Dashverse makes an
  hour of AI content for about $20,000 [9].
- FlexTV stopped live shooting entirely in 2026 [12].
- Holywater's AI app My Muse converts 23.6% of viewers to paying, against 23.9% for its
  live-action My Drama; 9 in 10 paying viewers watch past 100 minutes (company data [8]).
- ReelShort's CEO says AI's "market response is far better", but the company stays hybrid [10].

**Complaints.**
- Older Chinese animated drama was mocked as "PPT animation" [15]; Hongguo removed 3,522
  low-quality titles in one week (Apr 2026) [13].
- The hard part is keeping a character's identity and small acting (expression, pause, breath)
  across shots; viewers do call AI people "creepy" (GOKKO [31]). Named failure modes: identity
  drift, props appearing and vanishing, lips out of sync [29].
- One reviewer finished 3 of 25 AI titles: story decided it, fantasy worked best. **No
  representative survey of AI-drama viewers exists** [29].

**Rules.**
- China: visible plus embedded AI labels since 1 Sep 2025. Micro-drama measures in force since
  1 Sep 2026 require "prominent" AI labels and screening for repeated synthetic faces
  (secondary reporting [28]).
- YouTube: **animation and clearly unrealistic content need no AI disclosure**; realistic scenes
  and real people do [24]. Since 15 Jul 2025 it refuses to pay for "mass-produced, repetitious"
  content [27].
- TikTok: labels required on realistic AI content (secondary reporting).

## 6. Standalone drama on Shorts and TikTok

- Dhar Mann: underdog stories where bullies get their comeuppance and a moral is spoken [32]†;
  Fox signed him for a 40-title microdrama slate [10].
- Alan Chikin Chow: the most-watched Shorts creator, ~2bn views a month, a Short a day [33].
- GOKKO (Japan): 1-3 minute dramas, a new cast every story, scripts tuned on second-by-second
  retention data [31].

A Douyin story guide gives the standalone shape: set up an expectation, flip it with one line or
action, keep it believable, end on emotion [39].

No study isolates what makes a 60 s story satisfying, so this is practice. An episode is judged
by whether you tap "next"; a standalone by whether the last five seconds pay off a question the
first five asked. YouTube counts time spent waiting for an ending that never comes as no value
(guidebook rule 13). So keep the serial's hook, speed and suppression-then-release, and swap the
cliffhanger for a closing twist that recolours a planted detail.

**What does NOT transfer from 80-episode serials:**
- **The cliffhanger.** It exists to sell episode 11; here it reads as an unfinished film.
- **Paywall timing (卡点) and the slow burn.** A serial can humiliate its heroine for ten
  episodes; endless suffering without a counter is a listed mistake even there [16].
- **Stacked reversals.** One big twist plus two small turns; more is "Twist Addiction" [19].
- **Tropes that need world-building** (pack law, mafia families, rebirth rules). Pick a premise
  read in 3 s: a contract, a hidden identity, a wrong assumption.
- **Star pull.** 88% choose by the lead actor [11]†; here the character design and voice must
  sell the lead in 2 s.
- **Recaps, subplots, big casts.**

## Rules for a 60-second standalone vertical drama

Each rule can be checked on the script or the finished file. "Practice" marks common advice with
no hard source. "Guidebook" means `docs/shorts-guidebook.md`.

1. **Frame 0 is the conflict:** a face mid-emotion or the incident itself. No logo, title or
   establishing shot. [16][20]; guidebook rules 1 and 7.
2. **The first sound (a line or a hit) starts by 0:00.3; the first spoken line by 0:01.5.**
   Guidebook rule 2 (25 of 25 Shorts, median 0.07 s); GOKKO's 2 s hook [31].
3. **A muted viewer knows who is against whom and what is wrong by 0:03.** TikTok: the
   proposition in 3 s [23]; [16].
4. **Setup is finished by 0:10:** lead, opponent, want, stake. No backstory; causes come later
   or never. [16]
5. **The detail the twist will turn is shown clearly once by 0:12** (an object, a line, a status
   card). [21]; [39]
6. **The first reversal (the upper hand changes) lands by 0:25.** [16] ("before 30 s")
7. **No stretch longer than 20 s without a turn;** at least three turns in all (about 0:25,
   0:40 and 0:52). Scaled from [16] (every 20-30 s) and [19] (two spikes per 90 s).
8. **Humiliation lasts 10 s at most before payback starts.** [16] (endless suffering without
   a counter is a listed mistake); [11]
9. **The final twist lands between 0:48 and 0:56 and turns something planted.** Nothing new is
   introduced after 0:40. [21]; [19] (Twist Addiction)
10. **The question asked in the first 10 s is answered on screen.** No "to be continued", no
    cliffhanger. Guidebook rule 13; practice.
11. **It ends on a held image (face, hand or object) for 2-3 s, with the sting on or just after
    the last word.** No outro card. [21]; guidebook rule 28
12. **Two or three speaking characters, four at most; one location, two at most.** [20][19],
    scaled
13. **Every lead has a four-word conflict tag in the script and one visual tell that never
    changes between shots** (face, outfit, colour). [19]; [31][29] (identity drift is the most-named
    AI failure)
14. **A status card names each lead on first appearance** (name plus a status of 6 words or
    fewer, on screen for at least 1.2 s). Practice, unsourced (observed Chinese convention)
15. **No speech runs over two caption lines (about 15 words); the whole film has 90-150 spoken
    words.** [16] ("short, fragmented, sharp"); word count scaled from [19]
16. **Inner voice only where the picture cannot say it:** three lines at most, each with a named
    emotion, no backstory. [19][38]
17. **With the sound off, captions, cards and pictures still tell the whole story.** Captions
    are burned in from the first word. Guidebook rules 6 and 24; [23]
18. **A new picture (cut, camera move or new pose) every 2-4 s; nothing stands still for 3 s.**
    [18] (1.5-4.2 s average shot); guidebook rules 17 and 33
19. **Close-ups and medium close-ups make up at least half the shots; every reaction is a
    scripted beat.** [18][20]; the share is practice
20. **Each character has one distinct, steady voice and one caption colour or position.**
    Practice; [19] (stiff, machine-translated dialogue is a named failing)
21. **The premise is a trope a viewer reads in 3 s, and the twist turns the trope rather than
    repeating it.** [7] (tropes that sell); [34] (newer shows subvert); [11] (fatigue)
22. **No slapping as spectacle, no violence against women, no drugged or pregnancy plots,** and
    nothing Russian (people, brands, symbols). [11]; guidebook rule 32
23. **The film runs 55-65 s (Shorts allow up to 3 min [41]); captions sit between 67% and 83% of
    the height, nothing to read in the lower right strip.** Guidebook rules 25 and 26; over 60 s
    every sound is our own (rule 31)
24. **The file is 1080×1920, 30 fps, the characters visibly drawn.** House rule; [24] (animation
    needs no AI disclosure on YouTube)

### Beat sheet, 60 s

| Time | Beat | On screen and heard |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:02 | Cold open on the conflict | Hook line or hit by 0:00.3; lead in close-up; status card |
| 0:02-0:08 | Who wants what, against whom | Opponent's line; the stake in one sentence |
| 0:08-0:12 | Plant | The object or line the twist will turn, shown once, clearly |
| 0:12-0:20 | Pressure (suppression) | Opponent escalates; humiliation 10 s at most |
| 0:20-0:25 | First reversal (release) | Lead's counter or a reveal; the upper hand changes |
| 0:25-0:40 | Escalation | Opponent doubles down, or a new obstacle; second turn by 0:40 |
| 0:40-0:50 | Payoff | Status reversal lands; reaction close-ups |
| 0:50-0:56 | Final twist | One line or image recolours an earlier beat (the plant) |
| 0:56-1:00 | Button | Held image 2-3 s; sting on the last word; no outro card |

A channel's own Short adds the 4-5 s spoken subscribe ask after the button (guidebook rule 28).
