# KitCut runs your YouTube channel

The offer as of 2026-10-27, written after the first ten days of paid ads. We stop selling films
and start selling a channel: a steady run of videos that posts by itself, an assistant that reads
how each one did and decides what comes next, and an honest view of what the channel could earn.
Nothing here is built yet beyond what is listed under "What exists". Read it before designing
pricing, onboarding, the ad page or the assistant.

## Why the offer changes

The ads of 2026-10-16..27 tested two ways of selling films, and both failed for a reason a
channel offer fixes.

| | Oct 16-20: a free film, a $12 upsell | Oct 22-26: a card before anything |
|---|---|---|
| Ad spend | $196 | $683 |
| Sign-ups | 102 ($1.92 each) | 154 ($4.44 each) |
| Made a film | 68% | 5% |
| Started a trial | 0 | 9 (3 cards held) |
| Paying | 0 | 0 so far |

- A free film with a dismissable offer gave people no reason to pay (the "Remove branding" offer
  was shown 58 times: 3 clicks, 0 paid).
- A card before the first film stopped almost everyone before they saw anything.
- The ad trials that did start made one 30-second film on day one and never came back. The trial
  gave them 2 minutes of film and nothing to come back for.
- The people the ads bring searched for a faceless YouTube channel. A film is a step; a channel
  is what they came for.

## The offer in one line

**Your faceless YouTube channel: 10, 20 or 30 videos a month, posted on schedule, with Shorts
cut from each one, and a producer that watches what works and plans what's next.**

## What the customer buys

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Videos a month** | 10, 20 or 30. Each one its own script, research, narration and animation. |
| **Length** | They choose. Start at about 2 minutes; 8 minutes and longer earns more (YouTube puts mid-roll ads only in videos of 8 minutes or more). |
| **Shorts** | Three vertical Shorts cut from every video, for discovery. |
| **Posting** | Straight to their YouTube channel on a calendar, with titles, descriptions, chapters and four thumbnails each. |
| **The producer** | An assistant that reads every video's numbers at 2 and 7 days, says what worked and what didn't, and proposes the next videos with the reason for each. They approve with one click, or let it run. |
| **Everything else** | Premium voices, full HD, no KitCut mark, downloads, their own characters and places kept across the channel. |
| **Express (add-on)** | Videos made first and faster, for when they need a batch in a hurry. |

What competitors charge for comparable volume (pricing pages, 2026-10-27): VidRush $239 for 136
minutes a month; StoryShort $129 for about 24 long videos and $199 for about 50; Pictory $199 for
300 minutes of stock-footage editing; AutoShorts/Faceless.video $19-149 for Shorts only. Only
StoryShort pairs long videos with auto-posting, and none has a producer that plans from the
channel's own numbers. Our videos are written, researched and animated from nothing; theirs are
mostly stock clips and templates.

The minute-based plans stay for everyone who is not running a channel (invitations, business
explainers, templates). The channel plans are the path for the YouTube ads and the faceless page.

## How a customer gets there

1. **The ad and its page:** "Your faceless YouTube channel. 20 videos a month, posted for you."
2. **Pick a channel:** one of a dozen ready formats (the niches below), or their own idea in a line.
3. **The earnings view** (before sign-up): the niche's earnings per 1,000 views, and a slider of
   views per video with honest markers (see "What a channel can earn").
4. **Sign up with Google, no card.**
5. **The pilot, free (owner, 2026-10-27): a real 2-3 minute video,** the channel's first episode,
   at full quality, with our mark. The site estimates 35-55 minutes for 2 minutes and 45-70 for 3, so the page does not leave them
   waiting on a spinner: it moves straight on to step 6, and an email says when the video is ready.
6. **The whole picture, while it is made and again under the finished video:** the channel's name
   and look, 12 ideas for its next videos (a title and one line each, one Claude call), those
   ideas on a 2-week posting calendar, the earnings view for their niche, and a pick of length and
   videos a month with the price beside it.
7. **Then nothing more is free.** Watching and sharing the pilot's link stay open; downloading it,
   removing the mark, posting it to YouTube and every next video need the trial. The only button is
   **"Start my channel: 7 days free"**, with the card on the same page and the $1 check.
8. **Once the card holds:** the first 3 videos of the plan start making themselves and are
   scheduled to post. They come back to finished videos, not an empty page.
9. **Day 8:** the first charge, the rest of the month's videos, and the producer's first report.

**Who gets the pilot:** one per person (the existing one-person rules), and only sign-ups from the
countries the ads target; anyone else sees the plan and the trial without a free video. The page
says before they start: "Your first episode is free. Everything after it starts with a 7-day trial."

**What the pilot costs:** about $0.85-1.10 in cash on the Claude subscription ($5.60-7.20 at list
price), and the 12 ideas cents. The real limit is the subscription's room: at $20 a day of ads,
about 80 pilots a month use ~$500 of list-price Claude work, which fits; at $50 a day, ~200 pilots
use ~$1,300, about half of what one subscription carried in all of October. The 3 trial videos are
made only after a card holds, because two of three cards so far failed.

## The producer

This is the part nobody else sells, and the part we already do by hand for our own channels:
pick what people search for, make it, see what worked, make more of that.

**Every week, per channel:**

1. **Read** each video at 2 and 7 days: views, watch time, average view duration and percentage,
   subscribers gained, where the views came from, and the YouTube search terms that brought them.
2. **Judge** against the channel's own median, not against a fantasy: which topics, titles,
   lengths and openings did better, and which did worse.
3. **Look outward:** search demand for the topics near the winners (Google Ads keyword volume,
   YouTube's own search terms), what is thin on YouTube, what the niche is talking about now.
4. **Propose** next week's videos: a title, the angle, the length, and one line of why ("your
   video on X got 4x your median from search; 'Y' is searched 12,000 times a month and has two
   weak videos on it").
5. **Make and schedule** the approved ones, or all of them on autopilot.
6. **Report** in the Monday email: what happened, what it changed, what is coming.

**Rules it keeps:** no two videos on the same template; each has its own script, sources and
picture (YouTube does not pay for "inauthentic" mass-produced content and judges the whole
channel); realistic AI scenes of real people are disclosed; topics with violent detail are steered
away from, because they lose most ads.

**Run it on our own channels first.** Padel, research films and the AI news Shorts get the producer
before any customer does, and its proposals are measured against what we would have picked.

## What a channel can earn

YouTube pays only after a channel joins its Partner Program: 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 hours of
public watch time in a year (8,000 hours for channels applying from 1 February 2027), or 10 million
Shorts views in 90 days. Then earnings are views x RPM / 1,000, where RPM is what the creator keeps
per 1,000 views.

### Twelve niches (long videos, mostly US/UK/Canada/Australia viewers)

| Niche | RPM range | At 100,000 views a month |
|---|---|---|
| Personal finance | $5-17 | $500-1,700 |
| Real estate | $5-13 | $500-1,300 |
| Business | $4-9 | $400-900 |
| Tech & AI explained | $4-10 | $400-1,000 |
| Geography & maps | $2-12 | $200-1,200 |
| Education, how things work | $2-10 | $200-1,000 |
| Science & space | $2-10 | $200-1,000 |
| History | $2-10 | $200-1,000 |
| True crime & mysteries | $2-10 | $200-1,000 |
| Motivation | $2-10 | $200-1,000 |
| Psychology & health | $2-5 | $200-500 |
| Sports explained | $1-9 | $100-900 |

Shorts earn $0.05-0.30 per 1,000 views: they bring subscribers, not money.

Left off on purpose: kids' videos (RPM $0.33-1, plus child-safety rules), travel ($2-4), crypto
(no figure with a method behind it) and horror (ad and reused-content risk).

### The slider, and its honest markers

On the page the customer picks a niche, videos a month and a length, and moves one slider: views
per video. The slider carries three markers, each from data:

| Marker | Views per video | 20 videos a month | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| A new channel | ~50 | 1,000 views: no earnings yet, ~50 watch hours a month | median for channels under 1,000 subscribers (OverseerOS, 2026) |
| A small channel that found its feet | ~5,000 | 100,000 views: about 4,500 watch hours a month at a 2.7-minute average view, enough to qualify within a month or two | median for 1,000-9,900 subscribers |
| A hit | 50,000+ | one video like this can carry a month | the top 10% of videos take about half of all views |

The whole game is getting from the first marker to the second, and the only lever a creator
controls is shipping many good videos and learning from them. That is why the plans sell volume
and the producer.

### FAQ lines the page must carry

- **Why so many videos?** Most videos get few views and a few get most of them: on mature channels
  the top 10% of videos take about half of all views. Among 32.9 million small channels, 31% of
  those that posted 52 or more videos in a year reached 1,000 subscribers, against 2% of those that
  posted 1-11 (vidIQ, 2026; an association, not a promise).
- **When do I get paid?** Only after YouTube accepts the channel into its Partner Program. Many
  channels take months; some never qualify.
- **Will YouTube allow AI videos?** Yes, if each video is original and has real value. YouTube
  does not pay for mass-produced, templated or repetitive content, and it judges the channel as a
  whole. Every KitCut video has its own script, research and narration.
- **Are these earnings guaranteed?** No. RPMs are estimates from third parties (YouTube publishes
  none), and they move with the viewers' country, the season and the topic.

### What we never say

No "make $X a month", no income screenshots, no "passive income". A page that promises earnings
from an AI tool is exactly what the FTC has sued over (Operation AI Comply and the "AI-powered"
store cases since 2024). We show the facts above, the formula, typical results first, and the
caveats beside them, never below the fold.

## The business, per customer

Costs per video from production (2026-10): about $2 fixed + $0.026 a second at Claude's list price,
plus 8.5% for failed runs and edit rounds. In cash on the $200 Claude subscription a video costs
about 15% of that.

| Plan | Price | Our cost a month: cash | Our cost a month: list price |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 x 2 min | $49 | ~$8 | ~$56 |
| 20 x 5 min | $149 | ~$32 | ~$213 |
| 20 x 8 min | $219 | ~$54 | ~$360 |
| 30 x 8 min | $319 | ~$81 | ~$540 |

**On the subscription every plan earns; at list price every plan loses.** Two things decide which
is true:

1. **Claude capacity.** One $200 subscription carried about $2,700 a month of list-price work in
   October. A 20 x 8 customer is about $360 of it, so one subscription holds a handful of such
   customers beside everything else, and its real headroom has never been measured. Whether a
   subscription may power customer films at this scale is also unsettled.
2. **Cost per minute of a long video.** To sell 20 x 8 at $219 with a margin at list price, a
   minute of film has to cost about $1 instead of about $2.25. The levers to measure (never adopt
   unmeasured): a channel's characters and places reused from video to video, lower effort for
   scene work, a cheaper model for the parts a viewer cannot tell apart. The free pilot always gets
   the best model, because it is what sells the plan.

## Risks

- **Expectations.** A new channel's median video gets about 50 views. Customers who expected money
  in month one will cancel by month two or three. The page's markers and the producer's weekly
  report are the defence; churn is the number to watch after trials.
- **YouTube policy.** "Inauthentic content" is judged per channel, and YouTube removed big AI
  channels in January 2026. Variety and real value in every video are a product rule, not advice.
- **Claude cost and capacity,** above.
- **The YouTube app audit and upload quota,** above: set aside for now, not forgotten.
- **The 2027 Partner Program change:** from 1 February 2027 new channels need 8,000 watch hours,
  twice today's bar. The page must say which rule applies on the day a customer reads it.
