## 1. Origins and the Americas: Mexico, Argentina's 1980s–1990s boom and collapse, the recovery, Uruguay

### Takeaway
Padel began at Enrique Corcuera's house in Acapulco in 1969. It was a 1990s mass craze in Argentina: "3–4 million players" and "25,000+ courts" are the commonly quoted peak figures, but **no official statistics exist** for them. The boom collapsed in the late 1990s and early 2000s. The causes were oversupply, badly built courts, injuries and bad press, and the economic crisis that peaked in 2001, after which top players emigrated to Spain. A revival began around 2016 and accelerated during the pandemic. In 2025 Argentina had about 7,000 courts (FIP), and in 2026 it ranked third in the world for clubs.

### Cited Findings
**Origins (Mexico, 1969)**
- Enrique Corcuera built the first court at his Las Brisas house in Acapulco in 1969. His wife Viviana is credited with the first rules [WEAK, commercial club site] — [Corcuera Padel Club](https://www.corcuerapadelclub.com/blogs/about-padel/where-did-padel-come-from-the-story-of-enrique-corcuera-and-the-first-court-in-acapulco). The LTA's own history page gives the same 1969 Acapulco origin — [LTA Padel, History of padel](https://www.ltapadel.org.uk/what-we-do/what-is-the-history-of-padel/). Wikipedia gives 1968 as the year it was first played (use only as a pointer) — [Wikipedia, Padel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padel).
- The FIP history page describes the first court as 20 × 10 m, walled, with a net — [FIP, History](https://www.padelfip.com/history/).
- Mexico itself never had a 1980s court-building boom. Padel spread there first among affluent social circles [WEAK] — [padel-rules.com, Padel in Mexico](https://padel-rules.com/padel-around-the-world/padel-in-mexico/). I found no Mexican court counts for the 1980s or 1990s.

**Uruguay**
- The FIP says courts were built in Uruguay during the 1980s — [FIP, History](https://www.padelfip.com/history/). Another FIP article places the first Uruguayan courts in the late 1980s and early 1990s — [FIP, Oct 2024](https://www.padelfip.com/2024/10/from-argentina-and-spain-to-global-fame-the-padel-passion-that-conquered-the-world/).
- One source describes a 1983 showcase at the Zorba resort in Punta del Este with former Argentine Davis Cup players [WEAK] — [ipadel.co.uk](https://ipadel.co.uk/History-of-Padel-Tennis).
- Uruguay was one of the three founders of the FIP (Madrid, 12 July 1991, with Argentina and Spain) — [Tennis Giant timeline, WEAK](https://tennisgiant.com/blogs/all-about-padel/key-milestones-in-the-history-of-padel); [Wikipedia, FIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Padel_Federation).

**Argentina's 1990s peak (all figures unofficial)**
- La Nación (2022) wrote that the boom drew "more than 4 million players" and "more than 25,000 courts across the country in its years of glory" [PRESS] — [La Nación, "Fue boom en los 90…"](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/sabado/fue-boom-en-los-90-la-segunda-vida-del-deporte-que-marco-una-epoca-nid21012022/).
- An earlier La Nación piece put the peak at "about 3,000,000 players in 1992", explicitly "without official statistics" [PRESS] — [La Nación, "Padel: un deporte que intenta resurgir…"](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/deportes/padel-un-deporte-que-intenta-resurgir-tras-sus-gloriosos-anos-noventas-nid2097305/).
- Unidiversidad (UNCuyo, Mendoza) cites informal counts of about 3 million players and calls padel "the second most practised sport of the '90s". It says court construction "outran all planning" [PRESS] — [Unidiversidad](https://www.unidiversidad.com.ar/volvio-el-padel-tras-el-furor-de-los-90-el-deporte-renace-en-mendoza).
- El Equipo/Deportea says Argentina went from "almost three million players in the 90s" to about 500,000 [WEAK; the date of the 500,000 trough is not given] — [Deportea, Dec 2024](https://elequipo-deportea.com/2024/12/06/el-renacer-del-padel-en-argentina/).
- The "4.5 million players and 35,000 courts in 1993" figure circulates in encyclopaedic references, but **no locatable source** backs it (see Gaps).
- Padel is culturally tied to the Menem era (1989–99) "alongside the parripollo and the video club" [PRESS] — [Tiempo Argentino](https://www.tiempoar.com.ar/nota/redencion-presidencial-para-un-deporte-que-fue-boom-en-los-noventa); [DataClave](https://www.dataclave.com.ar/poder/el-padel--un-deporte-con-sello-menemista-que-regresa--que-politicos-juegan-_a6069d14e656b0833e6fbd4cb).
- First World Championship: September 1992 in Madrid and Seville. Argentina won both the men's and the women's titles [PRESS] — [El Gráfico](https://elgrafico.com.ar/articulo/maradona-y-el-grafico/54637/30-anios-maradona-su-llegada-a-sevilla-y-el-primer-mundial-de-padel).

**Why it collapsed**
- Courts closed in the 2001 crisis. Many clubs abandoned their courts (Mendoza) — [Unidiversidad](https://www.unidiversidad.com.ar/volvio-el-padel-tras-el-furor-de-los-90-el-deporte-renace-en-mendoza).
- Falling purchasing power led to falling activity and then a lack of business profitability. Tournaments stopped paying, so pros left for Spain. An ex-pro says there was "never a strong project to back it in times of crisis" — [DataClave](https://www.dataclave.com.ar/poder/el-padel--un-deporte-con-sello-menemista-que-regresa--que-politicos-juegan-_a6069d14e656b0833e6fbd4cb).
- Oversupply "made padel lose its mystique". There were mass injuries among players who started with no physical preparation, plus bad publicity [PRESS, regional] — [mnews](https://mnews.com.ar/deportes/21678-El-padel-volvio-para-quedarse-se-construye-mas-de-una-cancha-por-dia-).
- With no solid federation structure or regulation, low-quality courts were built indiscriminately [WEAK] — [Newsdigitales](https://www.newsdigitales.com/nota/328017/las-3-claves-del-declive-y-resurgimiento-del-padel-en-argentina/). On the old courts "the measurements were all different", according to an interviewee — [El Economista](https://eleconomista.com.ar/deportes/auge-padel-argentina-fenomeno-crecimiento-n91777).
- In the early 2000s, during the crisis under the De la Rúa government, many professionals emigrated to Spain — [El Economista](https://eleconomista.com.ar/deportes/auge-padel-argentina-fenomeno-crecimiento-n91777). In Buenos Aires city the sport "disappeared" while it survived in the interior — [El Economista](https://eleconomista.com.ar/deportes/auge-padel-argentina-fenomeno-crecimiento-n91777).

**Recovery (about 2016 onward)**
- In 2016 Infobae was already reporting a "resurgence of the 90s craze" — [Infobae, Nov 2016](https://www.infobae.com/tendencias/2016/11/10/el-resurgimiento-del-padel-el-deporte-furor-de-los-90/).
- The sport returned "at full pace in the middle of the pandemic" in Mendoza — [Los Andes, 14 Aug 2020](https://www.losandes.com.ar/mas-deportes/padel-en-mendoza-fue-furor-en-los-90-y-resurgio-a-todo-ritmo-en-medio-de-la-pandemia).
- Infobae (Feb 2021) estimated about 2 million players at 2,600 clubs with 4,900 courts [PRESS; the court and club figures trace to the booking app easycancha] — [Infobae, 28 Feb 2021](https://www.infobae.com/economia/2021/02/28/el-padel-volvio-con-todo-canchas-muy-modernas-y-profesionalizacion-las-claves-del-regreso-del-deporte-furor-de-los-90/); [Infonegocios](https://infonegocios.info/enfoque/el-negocio-del-padel-no-para-de-crecer-ya-genera-mas-reservas-que-el-tenis-cuantas-canchas-hay-en-argentina-y-en-cordoba).
- In 2022 "more than one court a day" was being built — [Premisa, 2022](https://www.premisa.com.ar/article/la_revancha_del_padel_se_construye_mas_de_una_cancha_por_dia_y_casi_no_hay_turnos_para_jugar2022526); [mnews](https://mnews.com.ar/deportes/21678-El-padel-volvio-para-quedarse-se-construye-mas-de-una-cancha-por-dia-).
- El Economista estimates 2–3 million players on 7,000–8,000 courts at about 2,600 clubs. The Asociación Argentina de Pádel says court numbers rose more than 30% in three years — [El Economista (business)](https://eleconomista.com.ar/deportes/boom-padel-argentina-negocio-rentable-n91778).
- FIP World Padel Report 2025 (June 2025 data): Argentina has about 7,000 courts, third in the world behind Spain and Italy — [studypadel table of FIP 2025, WEAK aggregator](https://www.studypadel.com/articles/padel-courts-worldwide).
- FIP World Padel Report 2026 (released Oct 2026): Argentina has 3,300 clubs, third behind Spain (4,705) and Italy (3,855) [PRESS reporting FIP] — [Ámbito](https://www.ambito.com/negocios/el-padel-mueve-us7930-millones-el-mundo-y-argentina-es-tercera-cantidad-clubes-n6334087); [Sitio Andino](https://www.sitioandino.com.ar/andino-sports/el-padel-supera-los-40-millones-jugadores-y-argentina-esta-los-paises-mas-clubes-n5731948).
- The Premier Padel Buenos Aires event (May 2025) sold more than 50,000 tickets and sold out — [La Nación, May 2025](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/deportes/padel/premier-padel-de-buenos-aires-mas-de-50000-entradas-vendidas-sold-out-y-un-impacto-exponencial-nid27052025/).
- In Santa Fe city there are now 27 padel complexes (municipal data) — [El Litoral](https://www.ellitoral.com/area-metropolitana/nostalgia-90-nuevo-furor-padel-volvio-boom-santa-fe_0_cLSD0yqnko.amp.html). Rosario has a "boom in federated players" — [La Capital](https://www.lacapital.com.ar/c10187575).

### Inferences
- For narration, the defensible line is "an estimated 3 to 4 million players and tens of thousands of courts at the 1990s peak, by unofficial counts". La Nación (2022: >4M, >25,000 courts) is the strongest press source, but it attributes no method. The "35,000 courts / 4.5M players (1993)" figure should not be used.
- Chartable Argentine court series (mixed methods, so label each point): ~25,000 at the 1990s peak (La Nación, unofficial) → 4,900 (2021, easycancha) → 7,000 (2025, FIP). The trough between them has no reliable figure.

### Gaps
- No official 1990s court or player census for Argentina, and no figure for the trough (about 2000–2010).
- I could not verify who first brought padel to Argentina, or when (often said to be Julio Menditeguy in the mid-1970s). No source surfaced.
- No Mexican or Uruguayan court or player counts over time. No founding date for the Federación Mexicana de Pádel.

## 2. Spain: from the Marbella jet set to a mass sport

### Takeaway
Padel came to Spain via Prince Alfonso de Hohenlohe's Marbella Club in 1974. It became a mass participation sport between 2005 and 2015. Federation licences rose from 6,137 in 2000 to 117,508 in 2025. In 2025 Spain had about 17,300 courts (FIP), the world's largest installed base. The claim that padel is "the second most practised sport in Spain" comes from the CSD's 2015 survey, and **only** for team or competitive sports (9% of the population vs football's 12%). The CSD 2022 survey is more striking. It found padel practised by 15.8% of those who did any sport, which put it **ahead of football (14.5%)** and 8th overall.

## 3. The European wave: Sweden, Italy, France, Portugal, UK, Netherlands, Belgium, Germany, Finland, Denmark

### Takeaway
Northern and western Europe took up padel in the late 2010s and boomed through the pandemic. Sweden is the cautionary tale. It went from about 717 courts (2019) to about 3,500 (2021), then busted in 2022–23. About 90 padel companies went bankrupt in 2023, more than 100 facilities closed in 2022–24, and unique court bookers fell from 540,000 (2021) to 421,000 (2022). Italy became number two in the world (about 1,000 courts in 2019, about 10,000 in 2025). The UK is the current hotspot: 68 courts in 2019, 1,553 at the end of 2025, and 1 million players by May 2026.

### Cited Findings

- 2019: 220 facilities, 717 courts (Svenska Padelförbundet + Matchi data) — [Padelfeber 2019](https://padelfeber.se/artiklar/2019/exakt-sa-stor-ar-nu-padeln-i-sverige/).
- End 2020: more than 500 halls with about 1,500 courts (federation's own membership application to the Swedish Sports Confederation) [PRIMARY] — [RF application](https://www.rf.se/download/18.407871d3183abb2a6131c1a/1665066578584/11.1%20Svenska%20Padelf%C3%B6rbundet.pdf).

### Inferences
- Sweden is the clearest boom-bust chart in the sport: courts ×5 in two years (717 in 2019 → ~3,500 in 2021, federation data), unique bookers −22% (540k → 421k, 2021→2022), then about 90 bankruptcies in 2023. Court totals then roughly plateaued at about 4,200 (FIP 2025), so the bust was in operators and occupancy, not in the national court count.
- Italy's slowdown (+8% in 2024) and Playtomic's 2026 "overcapacity" warning suggest the Swedish pattern was extreme but not unique.
- UK court numbers grew about 23× in six years (68 → 1,553). The player figures rest on a loose "played at least once in the year" definition.

### Gaps
- No Svenska Padelförbundet court count for 2022–2025, and no official number of Swedish halls closed (SVT is cited only second-hand).

- No official federation court counts for Portugal, Belgium or Germany. The NL series for 2021–2023 is missing.

## 4. Middle East and the rest of the world: Gulf, United States, Asia, Australia, Africa

### Takeaway
The Gulf picked up padel in the 2010s: the UAE about 2013 and Qatar in 2017–18. It is now the core of Asian padel, holding 72% of Asia's facilities per FIP 2025. Kuwait has the best courts-per-person ratio in Asia. Qatar, through Qatar Sports Investments, now owns the professional tour. The US is small but growing fast: 2,915 USPA members and 108 clubs (2025), about 1,000 courts by April 2026, and about 1.07M people who played at least once (SFIA 2025). Africa is led by Egypt and South Africa. Australia and China lag.

### Inferences
- **Which player number is most credible?** Neither global figure is a census. Playtomic's 19.4M (end 2025) is the more conservative, bottom-up estimate. The FIP's 35M/40M comes from a governing body pursuing Olympic inclusion, and its own report says only 1.11M players are registered with federations. A sum of the national survey figures gathered here gives a sanity check: Spain ~4.2M (2015 estimate), Italy ~2M, Argentina 2–3M (unofficial), US ~1.07M, GB 0.86–1M, NL 0.88M, Sweden 0.4–0.7M. That is about 11–13M across seven countries, which makes ~20M "regular" players worldwide plausible. 35–40M is plausible only with a loose "played at least once" definition. Recommended on-screen phrasing: "between about 20 million (Playtomic/PwC) and 40 million (FIP) players, depending on who counts and how".
- **Courts:** Playtomic's 58,334 (end 2025) is the best figure for commercially bookable club courts. FIP's 91,234 (2026) is the best for all courts, including private, hotel and residential ones. Both series show about 15–20% annual growth in 2022–2025.
- Chartable global court series (Playtomic, consistent method): ~40,000 (end 2022) → ~43,250 (end 2023) → 50,436 (end 2024) → 58,334 (end 2025). Sixfold since 2016 implies about 9,700 courts in 2016.

- No primary page was opened, so the report PDFs' tables (country breakdowns, especially Playtomic 2026 and FIP 2026) are unverified.
- The 2022 Playtomic/Deloitte first edition's headline court and player totals were not found.
- No year-by-year FIP membership count for 1991–2020 from a primary source.

## 6. The COVID-19 effect

### Takeaway
Padel is widely said to have benefited from the pandemic: outdoor courts, a small group, distance from opponents, and other sports closed. National data show the surge. Swedish courts more than doubled in 2021, Spanish licences rose 28% from 2020 to 2021, UK courts rose 72% in 2021, and the Argentine revival accelerated mid-2020. However, **no rigorous study isolates a pandemic effect**, and growth was already under way before 2020.

### Cited Findings
- Spanish licences: 75,548 (2020) → 96,872 (2021), +28% — [elneverazo](https://www.elneverazo.com/la-federacion-espanola-de-padel-registra-un-nuevo-record-de-licencias/); [Cluster Pádel](https://www.clusterpadel.com/noticias-padel/20231107/federacion-espanola-padel-sobrepasa-barrera-historica-100000-licencias.aspx).
- Sweden: about 1,500 courts (end 2020) → about 3,500 (2021). Unique bookers rose by more than 100,000 in a year to 540,000 (2021) — [RF application](https://www.rf.se/download/18.407871d3183abb2a6131c1a/1665066578584/11.1%20Svenska%20Padelf%C3%B6rbundet.pdf); [Padeldirekt, Nov 2021](https://padeldirekt.se/2021/11/26/farsk-statistik-fran-svenska-padelforbundet-540-000-svenskar-har-bokat-en-padelbana-2021). Sweden's boom ran about 2016–2020 and "the pandemic accelerated it" [WEAK] — [CourtBrain](https://www.courtbrain.com/articles/what-happened-to-padel-in-sweden).
- GB courts: 87 (2020) → 150 (2021) → 220 (2022) — [LTA](https://www.ltapadel.org.uk/news/padels-rapid-rise-great-britain-reaches-1000-padel-courts/).
- Netherlands: 475 courts in 2020, more than tripled since 2021 — [KNLTB/EY](https://www.knltb.nl/media/xvtejmk4/eyxknltb_padel_in_cijfers-2025.pdf).
- Argentina: padel "resurgió a todo ritmo en medio de la pandemia" (Mendoza, Aug 2020) — [Los Andes](https://www.losandes.com.ar/mas-deportes/padel-en-mendoza-fue-furor-en-los-90-y-resurgio-a-todo-ritmo-en-medio-de-la-pandemia).
- Global: courts +28% (+8,200) in 2022, the strongest year in the Playtomic series — [PR Newswire, 2023 report](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/playtomic-and-monitor-deloitte-report-the-padel-industry-will-grow-to-reach-6-billion-euros-in-2026-301861180.html).
- Commentary: padel was an "unexpected beneficiary" of lockdowns, as enclosed courts allowed contained social contact. Closures of other sports pushed people to padel [WEAK] — [The Padel Post](https://thepadelpost.substack.com/p/the-exhilarating-world-of-padel); [Padel Magazine, "The boom of padel in the post-Covid period"](https://padel-magazine.co.uk/the-boom-of-padel-during-the-post-covid-period/).
- One market report puts the global padel market at about $4bn (2020) → about $14bn (2025) and dates the inflection to about 2018, before COVID [WEAK, unverified] — [LSC Research](https://research.leaguesportsco.com/reports/the-padel-boom-2026/).

### Inferences
- The defensible framing is "the pandemic accelerated a boom already under way". Swedish facilities were already rising before 2020 (98 in 2017 → 508 in 2020, secondary), and Spain's licences grew steadily from 2009. The post-COVID hangover (Sweden 2022–23) is part of the same story.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or official study measuring padel's pandemic effect. No monthly booking data across 2020–21 from Playtomic or Matchi was found.

## 7. Notable firsts, big venues and celebrities

### Takeaway
The milestones that are sourced: first court (Acapulco 1969); first in Europe (Marbella 1974); FIP founded (Madrid 1991); first World Championship (Madrid/Seville 1992); first glass court (Mar del Plata 1989, single vendor source); first multi-sport games (European Games, Kraków, June 2023); first Premier Padel event (Doha, March 2022); Foro Italico (May 2022); Roland-Garros Philippe-Chatrier (July 2022). **There is no evidence of padel at the Bernabéu.** The only 2026 Bernabéu racket event was a tennis practice court. Footballers (Zlatan, Messi, Ronaldo, Beckham, Robben, Zidane) are tied to the boom as players, investors and faces.
