## 1. National federations: when the Argentine, Spanish, Mexican, Uruguayan and other early bodies were founded

### Takeaway
Argentina had the first national body, the Asociación Argentina de Pádel (APA). It is usually dated 1988, though one press source says 1987. Spain's association followed in 1991, in time to co-found the FIP with Argentina and Uruguay that July. Mexico's federation (Femepa) dates from 27 April 1992. I found no reliable founding date for the Federación Española de Pádel as a federation, or for the Uruguayan body.

### Cited Findings
- **Origin of the sport (context):** FIP places the sport's invention in 1969 in Acapulco, Mexico, at Enrique Corcuera's house in Las Brisas. — [FIP, historia (ES)](https://www.padelfip.com/es/historia/); [FIP "De Argentina y España a la fama mundial" (Oct 2024)](https://www.padelfip.com/es/2024/10/de-argentina-y-espana-a-la-fama-mundial-el-fenomeno-del-padel-que-conquisto-el-mundo/)
- **Argentina, 1988:** A group of friends led by Oscar "Cacho" Nicastro founded the Asociación Argentina de Pádel (APA), and Alberto Villaverde was its first president. FIP calls it the world's first national association ("Asociación Pádel Argentino", 1988). — [FIP, historia (ES)](https://www.padelfip.com/es/historia/); [Red Bull, historia del pádel](https://redbull.com/es-es/historia-del-padel)
- **Argentina, conflicting date of 1987:** Tiempo Argentino describes Mauricio Macri as a founding member of the Asociación de Pádel Argentina in **1987**. — [Tiempo Argentino](https://www.tiempoar.com.ar/nota/redencion-presidencial-para-un-deporte-que-fue-boom-en-los-noventa)
- **Naming varies:** Sources call the Argentine body "Asociación Argentina de Pádel", "Asociación Pádel Argentino" and "Asociación de Pádel Argentino". All abbreviate it APA. — [Red Bull](https://redbull.com/es-es/historia-del-padel); [FIP ES history](https://www.padelfip.com/es/historia/)
- **Spain, 1991:** The Asociación Española de Pádel was founded in 1991. It was officially registered under the acronym P.A.D.E.L., for "promoción de actividades deportivas, educativas y lúdicas". The same passage appears in an FIP press document. — [FIP, historia (ES)](https://www.padelfip.com/es/historia/); [FIP PDF "Ascensión del pádel" (10 Oct 2024)](https://padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/10-de-octubre_Ascension_del-padel_ESP.pdf)
- **Uruguay:** Uruguay's national body was one of the three that founded the FIP in Madrid on 12 July 1991, so it existed by then. Its own founding date was not found. — [FIP history](https://www.padelfip.com/history/)
- **Mexico, 1992:** The Federación Mexicana de Pádel (Femepa) was founded on **27 April 1992** and joined the FIP the same year. — [FIP "Focus en… México" (Mar 2025)](https://www.padelfip.com/es/2025/03/focus-en-mexico-donde-el-padel-ha-superado-el-millon-de-practicantes/)
- **Mexico today:** The same FIP piece says Mexico has passed one million players. — [FIP "Focus en… México"](https://www.padelfip.com/es/2025/03/focus-en-mexico-donde-el-padel-ha-superado-el-millon-de-practicantes/)

### Inferences
- The founding order is Argentina (1987/88), Spain (association, 1991), the FIP (July 1991), then Mexico (1992). Nothing here dates a separate Uruguayan founding.
- Spain's 1991 body is described as an "asociación". The move to a "Federación Española de Pádel" recognised by Spain's sports authorities came later, but I could not date it.

## 2. The International Padel Federation (FIP): founding, presidents, membership, recognitions, rules

### Takeaway
The FIP was founded on 12 July 1991 at the Club de Golf La Moraleja in Madrid by the Argentine, Spanish and Uruguayan associations. Its first president was Julio Alegría Artiach of Bilbao. The Italian Luigi Carraro was elected in November 2018 and re-elected in 2021 and October 2024. Membership grew from about 32 national federations (2019) to **115 (12 October 2026)**. Recent recognitions came from FISU (Feb 2025) and the Olympic Council of Asia (Nov 2025). I found no confirmation of IOC recognition.

## 3. The World Padel Championships

### Takeaway
The first World Championship was held in Spain in 1992 (Madrid and Seville), with the finals at La Cartuja during Expo 92. Argentina won both men's and women's titles, Spain took silver and Uruguay bronze. The event is roughly biennial. To 2024, Argentina has 12 men's titles and Spain 4. Spain's women have won six in a row (2014–2024) and nine in all. The 2026 edition, renamed the "FIP World Cup", is scheduled for Doha on 2–7 November 2026 and **has not been played as of 16 October 2026**.

## 4. The professional tours: Padel Pro Tour, World Padel Tour, Premier Padel, CUPRA FIP Tour, A1 Padel, Hexagon Cup, and prize money

### Takeaway
The professional tours ran in this sequence:
1. The Beefeater Circuit.
2. The **Padel Pro Tour** (founded 2005, seasons 2006–2012).
3. The **World Padel Tour** (2013–2023), owned by Damm through Setpoint Events.
4. **Premier Padel**, launched in 2022 by FIP with QSI money and PPA backing, which led to a two-year legal war with WPT.

On 24 August 2023 QSI agreed to buy WPT from Damm. From 2024 there has been one elite tour, Premier Padel, under FIP governance; in 2026 it has 26 events in 18 countries. The FIP's CUPRA FIP Tour is the feeder, and the team format (Hexagon Cup, becoming the Hexagon World Series in 2027) is growing alongside. Prize money rose from about €110k for a top WPT final event (2022) to €525k for a 2022 Premier Padel Major and $750k per Major in 2026. The season-total figures are weakly sourced.

## 5. Multi-sport Games and the Olympic question

### Takeaway
Padel's multi-sport debut as a medal sport at a major continental Games came at the **2023 European Games in Kraków**. It was preceded, per FIP, by the 2022 South American Games. In 2026 it added the **Mediterranean Games (Taranto, Aug 2026)** and the **Asian Games (Aichi-Nagoya, 28 Sep–3 Oct 2026)**. I found no evidence it is on the Lima 2027 Pan American Games programme. Padel is **not an Olympic sport** as of October 2026: it was not at Paris 2024 and is not at LA 2028. The Brisbane 2032 programme is due in 2027. FIP's IOC-recognition status is unconfirmed.

## 6. Champions over time: number-one pairs by era and the records

### Takeaway
- **Men:** Lasaigues (with Álvarez Clementi, then Gattiker) ruled the early 1990s. Then came the longest reign in the sport: Belasteguín and Juan Martín Díaz were world number one for **13 consecutive seasons (2002–2014)** and won **170 titles together**. Belasteguín extended his own run to **16 years** with Pablo Lima. After that the top spot went to Sanyo Gutiérrez and Maxi Sánchez (2018), Paquito Navarro and Juan Lebrón (2019), Galán and Lebrón (2020 to May 2023), then **Coello and Tapia (May 2023 to now)**.
- **Women:** Carolina Navarro (nine years at number one, much of it with Cecilia Reiter). The WPT era rotated between the Sánchez Alayeto twins, Marrero and Salazar, Triay and Salazar, and Ari Sánchez and Paula Josemaría (2023–2024). **Gemma Triay and Delfi Brea** have held number one since May 2025.
