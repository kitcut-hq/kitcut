## 1. The court: from Corcuera's walled yard to the panoramic glass court

### Takeaway
The founding layout survives almost unchanged. The FIP's own history says Corcuera's 1969 court in Acapulco was 20 × 10 m inside 3 m walls, and the 2026 FIP rules still say 20 × 10 m with 3 m walls plus 1 m of mesh at the ends. What changed is the material and the look. Concrete or masonry walls on a cement floor gave way to glass (it spread in the 1990s and took over later as it got cheaper), then to artificial turf, then to the pillar-less "panoramic" TV court. The first panoramic tour court is dated to the 2013 World Padel Tour, but that date rests on its manufacturer's claim. Pro courts then turned blue, with a black-turf episode in 2015.

### Cited Findings
**Origins (Corcuera, Hohenlohe)**
- Enrique Corcuera, a tennis fan without room for a full tennis court at his home in Las Brisas, Acapulco, built the first court in **1969**. The FIP history gives it as **20 × 10 m, enclosed by 3 m walls, with a tennis net in the centre** — [FIP, Historia (ES)](https://www.padelfip.com/es/historia/); [FIP, History (EN)](https://www.padelfip.com/history/)
- Some secondary sources say the walls were **3 to 4 m** high — [tumejorpala.com [weak]](https://tumejorpala.com/historia-del-padel)
- **Date conflict.** Some secondary sources give **1962** and describe Corcuera adapting a frontón (pelota) court rather than building from scratch — [padelspain.net [weak]](https://www.padelspain.net/material/10549/Evolucion-de-las-palas-de-padel-de-la-madera-al-carbono/); [padelagogo.com [weak]](https://www.padelagogo.com/la-evolucion-de-las-pistas-de-padel/). The FIP, the LTA and most other sources say 1969 — [FIP](https://www.padelfip.com/history/); [LTA Padel](https://www.ltapadel.org.uk/what-we-do/what-is-the-history-of-padel/)
- A secondary site citing the FIP says his wife **Viviana** wrote the first rules as a birthday gift — [studypadel.com [weak]](https://www.studypadel.com/es/articulos/historia-del-padel)
- One secondary account says Corcuera kept tennis's scoring system and net height and added the walls as part of play — [padel-rules.com [weak]](https://padel-rules.com/history-and-culture/history-of-padel/)
- **Alfonso de Hohenlohe** saw the game in Mexico and built **Spain's first two courts at the Marbella Club**. Most sources give **1974**; the FIP history says "early 1970s" — [LTA Padel](https://www.ltapadel.org.uk/what-we-do/what-is-the-history-of-padel/); [padel.ac [weak]](https://www.padel.ac/history-of-padel/); [FIP History](https://www.padelfip.com/history/)
- **Argentina.** One account says an Argentine millionaire who was a Marbella regular (named elsewhere as **Julio Menditeguy**) took it there in **1975**, i.e. the mid-1970s. Another timeline says **1982** — [studypadel.com [weak]](https://www.studypadel.com/es/articulos/historia-del-padel); [padel-rules.com [weak]](https://padel-rules.com/history-and-culture/history-of-padel/)
- **Hohenlohe's standardisation.** I found no source that gives specific dimensions or rules Hohenlohe changed. See Gaps.

**Walls and floor: concrete to glass to turf**
- The first courts were laid on a **cement slab, with no artificial grass and no glass** — [GIMPADEL, court builder [weak]](https://gimpadel.com/2018/10/26/evolucion-pistas-padel/)
- Early courts were modified frontones; purpose-built courts with **concrete walls** came later — [padelagogo [weak]](https://www.padelagogo.com/la-evolucion-de-las-pistas-de-padel/); [acepadshop [weak]](https://acepadshop.com/en/blogs/noticias/historia-del-padel-del-muro-al-cristal-y-pista-negra-wpt-y-premier-padel)
- For the first years only the **wall (masonry) court** existed. **Glass courts spread through the 1990s**, but they only took over years after their introduction, once they got cheaper, and masonry courts then stopped being built. **Mixed wall-and-glass courts** appeared in South America in the **late 1980s, and mostly in the early 1990s** — [Ferrobalpadel, court builder [weak]](http://ferrobalpadel.com/pistas-de-padel-de-muros-vs-pista-de-padel-de-cristal/)
- A padel blog puts the jump to glass and panoramic courts in **2000–2010**. It says early glass courts needed many more pillars and reinforcements than modern panoramic ones — [padelagogo [weak]](https://www.padelagogo.com/la-evolucion-de-las-pistas-de-padel/). This conflicts with the "1990s" date above. The likely reading is that glass arrived in the 1990s and became dominant in the 2000s, but no source states that directly.
- **Turf.** Padel "took its first steps on concrete or cement". Courts then laid **artificial grass** for comfort. Nylon fibres were used first and replaced by **polypropylene** because nylon was abrasive. **No source gave a date** for this — [padelazo.com [weak]](https://www.padelazo.com/2020/05/cesped-artificial-para-pistas-de-padel/); [padelstar.es [weak]](https://padelstar.es/pistas-padel/tipos-de-pistas-de-padel-materiales-superficies-y-combinaciones-curiosas/)
- On the World Padel Tour (WPT) centre court the turf was **Mondo Supercourt, 10 mm** — [Padel10, manufacturer [weak/commercial]](https://padel10.com/pistas-de-padel/top-10)

**The panoramic (pillar-less) court**
- What makes a court panoramic is that the end walls have no pillars or frames. The design was born for TV — [adpadel [weak]](https://adpadel.com/panoramica/); [superprof [weak]](https://www.superprof.es/blog/pistas-padel-estilos/). In the "super-panoramic" variant the glass panels are joined only by small metal rods — [padelhispania [weak/commercial]](https://www.padelhispania.com/en/padel-courts/full-panoramic/)
- **The first panoramic tour court (manufacturer's claim).** The manufacturer Padel10 says it was chosen at the **end of 2012** to build the first WPT court. The tour asked for a court that was **portable and fully panoramic**. Its predecessor, the Padel Pro Tour, had a good portable court that was **not panoramic**. Padel10 says the court debuted in **2013 in Murcia**, at the WPT's first event — [Padel10, "La primera pista de pádel World Padel Tour" [commercial, single source]](https://padel10.com/la-primera-pista-de-padel-world-padel-tour.html)
- The same manufacturer says that **since 2017** the tour court's side walls have used **two large 2 × 3 m glass panels** — [Padel10 TOP10 [commercial]](https://padel10.com/pistas-de-padel/top-10)
- The **Padel Pro Tour ran 2006–2012**. The **World Padel Tour ran 2013–2023**, opening with 19 tournaments in 2013. **Premier Padel started in 2022** — [Wikipedia, Padel Pro Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padel_Pro_Tour); [Wikipedia, 2013 World Padel Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_World_Padel_Tour); [Wikipedia, Premier Padel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_Padel)

**Court colour (the blue court)**
- Courts of the 1990s and early 2000s were **almost all green**, a carry-over from grass tennis and club aesthetics — [actu-padel.com [weak]](https://actu-padel.com/en/padel-court-why-has-blue-become-the-standard-color-in-padel/)
- The **WPT was the first major tour to make blue the visual standard**. Blue is credited to TV, because it contrasts with the yellow ball — [actu-padel.com [weak]](https://actu-padel.com/en/padel-court-why-has-blue-become-the-standard-color-in-padel/)
- **The black-turf episode, in two versions:**
  - Version A: the WPT first used **black turf at an outdoor event in Valladolid, summer 2015**; heat and glare problems sent it back to blue — [actu-padel.com [weak]](https://actu-padel.com/en/padel-court-why-has-blue-become-the-standard-color-in-padel/)
  - Version B (the turf supplier's case study): the fibre colour changed from **blue in 2014 to black in 2015**, for aesthetics — [Mondo, "World Padel Tour: History" [commercial]](https://www.mondoworldwide.com/emea/en/spaziomondo/projects/project-world-padel-tour/)
- One source says the FIP approves **blue, green and earth-brown** turf, with black and fuchsia gaining popularity — [padelgest.com [weak]](https://padelgest.com/en/blue-green-or-earth-toned-padel-court/)

**The regulation court today (FIP Rules of Padel, in force from 1 Jan 2026)**
- **Court:** a rectangle **10 m wide × 20 m long (interior measurements), ±0.5% tolerance** — [FIP Rules of Padel 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf)
- **Net:** **10 m long, 0.88 m high at the centre, rising to 0.92 m at the ends**, max tolerance **0.005 m**. It hangs on a metal cable of max **0.01 m** diameter between posts of max **1.05 m**. The mesh is synthetic fibre, woven tight enough that the ball cannot pass — [FIP Rules of Padel 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf)
- **End walls:** **4 m total height, the first 3 m wall** of "any transparent or solid material (crystal, bricks, etc.)", **the last 1 m metallic fence** — [FIP Rules of Padel 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf). The rules still allow brick in 2026, which helps the film's "then vs now" framing.
- **Glass standard:** the 2023 FIP text requires tempered glass to **EN 12150-1** in the EU, and tells other countries to consult their own standards. The 2024 text points to an **FIP homologation annex** instead — [FIP game regulations 2023](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2-game-regulations.pdf); [FIP Rules 2024 upload](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/FIP-Rules-of-Padel.pdf)
- **Free height above the court:** minimum **6 m** over the whole court with no obstructions such as spotlights; **8 m suggested** for new facilities — [FIP Rules of Padel 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf)
- **Openings and out-of-court zone (2026):** **two access points per side**. Outside the court there must be no obstacles in an area at least **3 m wide (4 m recommended) × 4 m long on either side × 3 m high**. Players may only play from outside where these conditions are met — [FIP Rules of Padel 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf)
- **Older out-of-court minimum (version cited by a blog):** **2 m high × 8 m long (4 m each side of the net) × 2 m wide**, with "one or two openings per side, with or without a door", and handles on the outside — [padelstar.es, citing the reglamento [weak]](https://padelstar.es/reglas-de-padel/reglamento-juego-autorizado-fuera-de-la-pista/). If both are accurate, the safety zone got bigger by 2026. That needs checking against the PDFs.
- The search extracts note that the **2023 FIP text describes the stepped side walls differently** from the 2026 text, and that the 2026 text gives the wall-and-mesh layout in two variants. I could not retrieve the exact side-wall numbers — [FIP game regulations 2023](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2-game-regulations.pdf)

### Inferences
- The founding size (20 × 10 m, 3 m walls), the tennis-derived net and the scoring have stayed stable for more than 55 years. The film can honestly say "the footprint hasn't changed since 1969". The materials (cement and concrete → glass → panoramic glass; bare cement → nylon turf → polypropylene turf) and the TV look (green → blue, black briefly in 2015) are where the "then vs now" contrast lives.
- "Indoor courts" and the 6 m / 8 m clear-height rule (no lights in the playing volume) follow from padel moving indoors. I found no dated source for when indoor courts became common.

### Gaps
- **Hohenlohe's "standardisation"**: no source said what he changed, such as the mesh or the stepped side walls. Do not narrate a specific change without a source.
- **The exact side-wall profile in 2026** (glass heights and the step positions along the sides) and **when the 4 m back (3 m wall + 1 m mesh) was fixed**: not retrieved. The FIP PDF needs to be opened.
- **A date for glass replacing masonry**: only court builders and blogs, which conflict (1990s vs 2000–2010).
- **A date for artificial grass**: none found.
- **The panoramic court**: the 2013 WPT Murcia debut comes only from the manufacturer that built it. No independent press source was found, and I found nothing on whether earlier panoramic courts existed outside the tour.
- **Indoor courts**: no dated history found.
- Wikipedia, padelfip.com and the Spanish press (Marca, AS, El País) could not be opened in this session. Marca, AS, El País and The Guardian are also blocked from the search tool's crawler.

## 2. The racket (pala): from wood to carbon, and the regulation limits

### Takeaway
The racket went from solid wood (about 450 g, about 10 mm thick) to rubber/EVA-cored paddles in the late 1980s to 1990s (about 390 g by the late 1990s), then to fibreglass and carbon faces over a foam or EVA core from the 2000s. The history before the rules comes only from shops and blogs. The 2026 FIP limits are well sourced to the rulebook: total length ≤ 45.5 cm, width ≤ 26 cm, thickness ≤ 38 mm, a mandatory non-elastic wrist cord ≤ 35 cm, and **no weight limit**.

### Cited Findings
**History (all [weak], from shop and blog histories; flag them as such in the film)**
- The first rackets were **wood**: on average **about 450 g**, hard and uncomfortable, max thickness around **10 mm** — [padelspain.net [weak]](https://www.padelspain.net/material/10549/Evolucion-de-las-palas-de-padel-de-la-madera-al-carbono/); [newpadel.net [weak/shop]](https://www.newpadel.net/blog/evolucion-palas-de-padel/); [webdepadel.com [weak/shop]](https://webdepadel.com/historia-y-evolucion-de-las-palas-de-padel/)
- One source says Corcuera played with **Marcraft** wooden paddles. This is a single, unverified source — [padelpro.news / shooterpadel [weak]](https://padelpro.news/i/w910jspynmlkyxsoyfxnr5d2)
- **Who first used rubber (EVA)? Conflict:**
  - The Argentine brand **SANE** was the first to build a padel racket with "goma EVA" — [padelspain.net / newpadel [weak]](https://www.newpadel.net/blog/evolucion-palas-de-padel/)
  - The rubber paddle was invented by **Carlos Pérez in Buenos Aires in 1989**, the source claiming an international prior-art search found no match — [padelandia.com, "Historia oficial sobre la paleta de goma" [weak, self-styled "official"]](https://www.padelandia.com/padel/la-paleta-de-goma/)
- The rubber core cut the vibration reaching the elbow and lightened the racket, **from about 450 g to about 390 g by the late 1990s** — [padelspain.net [weak]](https://www.padelspain.net/material/10549/Evolucion-de-las-palas-de-padel-de-la-madera-al-carbono/)
- **Fibreglass and carbon from the 2000s.** Fibreglass sits in cheaper mid- and low-range rackets and carbon in power rackets; today both are often combined over a **FOAM or EVA** core — [padelspain.net [weak]](https://www.padelspain.net/material/10549/Evolucion-de-las-palas-de-padel-de-la-madera-al-carbono/); [seriesnacionalesdepadel [weak]](https://seriesnacionalesdepadel.wordpress.com/2020/05/17/historia-evolucion-palas-padel/)
- A Spanish utility-model patent exists for a "shock-absorbing padel racket" with many holes in the head. It does not establish when holes first appeared — [Google Patents ES1063992U](https://patents.google.com/patent/ES1063992U/es)
- **Shapes (descriptive only, no dates found):**
  - **Round:** large, low, central sweet spot; control and beginners
  - **Teardrop:** sweet spot higher; the middle option
  - **Diamond:** weight toward the tip; power, smaller sweet spot, more arm strain
  - Sources: [Red Bull](https://www.redbull.com/int-en/how-to-choose-a-padel-racket); [padelandtennis.co.uk [weak/shop]](https://www.padelandtennis.co.uk/guides/padel-racket-shapes-explained)

**Regulation now (FIP Rules of Padel 2026)**
- "The length of the head plus the length of the handle may not exceed **45.5 cm**, maximum width **26 cm**, maximum thickness **38 mm**" — [FIP Rules of Padel 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf); [FIP 2024 upload](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/FIP-Rules-of-Padel.pdf)
- **Handle:** max length **20 cm**, max width at the throat **50 mm** (not counting the open space), max thickness **50 mm** — [FIP Rules of Padel 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf)
- **Holes:** an unlimited number of **cylindrical holes of 9–13 mm** in the central area. Near the edge (within about 4 cm) holes may be larger or differently shaped, **up to 20 mm** — [FIP Rules (search extract)](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf). Some 2026 explainers present a "20 mm hole limit" as new for 2026; that is unconfirmed — [Pelotista [weak]](https://pelotista.com/blog/padel-rules)
- **Wrist cord:** a **non-elastic cord, max 35 cm**, fixed to the handle and worn around the wrist; use is **obligatory** — [FIP Rules of Padel 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf)
- **Measurement tolerance:** **2.5%** on thickness at inspection. This comes from secondary sources, not the FIP text I could see — [@padel on Threads [weak]](https://www.threads.com/@padel/post/DRFNrYpjFiM/padel-rackets-must-follow-specific-size-rules-set-by-the-official-fip-regulation); [padel.how [weak]](https://padel.how/rules/official-fip-padel-rules/)
- **Weight:** the rulebook extracts show **no weight limit**. A blog quotes **340–390 g** as the typical range today — [aceonepadel [weak]](https://aceonepadel.com/blogs/news/padel-racket-thickness-38mm-2026)
- **Cord penalty (2026, unconfirmed):** Spanish blogs say that from 2026, if the cord breaks or the racket flies out of the hand, the point is lost immediately — [padelbarcelona.es [weak]](https://padelbarcelona.es/padel/nuevas-normas-padel-2026-reglamento-fip-guia-completa/); [pala-hack.com [weak]](https://pala-hack.com/blog/cambios-normativa-padel-2026/). A search limited to the FIP domain found **nothing** confirming this.

### Inferences
- Weight: about 450 g (wood) → about 390 g (late-1990s rubber) → about 340–390 g (modern composites). The weight has hardly dropped since the late 1990s. What changed is the materials and the balance (the shapes). Every weight figure is from [weak] sources, so frame them as "typical", not regulated.
- In the 2026 rules the 38 mm thickness limit is roughly four times the ~10 mm of an early wooden paddle. That is a strong visual if the film says "about", since the 10 mm figure is single-sourced.

### Gaps
- **When the 45.5 / 26 / 38 mm limits were first written into the FIP rules**, and whether earlier editions had other numbers: not found.
- **When each shape (round, teardrop, diamond) arrived**, and who introduced it: not found in any source. Manufacturer histories (Bullpadel, Head, Babolat, Adidas) were not reachable.
- **When holes first appeared**, and when hole-size limits entered the rules: not found.
- **When EVA/foam replaced solid wood**: 1989 (Argentina) is a single [weak] claim, and the SANE claim has no date.

## 3. The ball: specification vs a tennis ball

### Takeaway
A padel ball has the same weight band as a tennis ball (56.0–59.4 g). It is slightly smaller (6.35–6.77 cm), it has a lower bounce ceiling (135–145 cm from a 2.54 m drop, against roughly 135–147 cm for tennis), and it has lower internal pressure. The FIP writes that pressure as "4.6–5.2 kg per 2.54 square cm"; secondary sources give about 10–11 psi for padel against about 12–14 psi for tennis. I found no source for when padel-specific balls first appeared.

### Cited Findings
- **FIP spec (Rules of Padel, 2026):** diameter **6.35–6.77 cm**, weight **56.0–59.4 g**. A new ball must bounce **135–145 cm** when dropped onto a hard surface from **2.54 m**. Internal pressure "between **4.6 kg and 5.2 kg per 2.54 square cm**" — [FIP Rules of Padel (In force 1.01.2026)](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/FIP-Rules-of-Padel.pdf); [FIP Rules 2026 review](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf)
- **The pressure figure is ambiguous.** "Per 2.54 square cm" is probably a garbled "per square inch" (2.54 cm = 1 inch). Read literally it is about 65–74 psi, which cannot be right for a ball. Secondary sources restate it as **about 10–11 psi** ("4.6–5.2 kg per square inch"), or **about 24.7–25.7 psi absolute** once atmospheric pressure is added — [networldsports [weak/shop]](https://www.networldsports.com/buyers-guides/padel-balls-guide); [zonadepadel.es [weak]](https://www.zonadepadel.es/blog/2020/04/cual-es-la-correcta-presion-de-las-pelotas-de-padel/). Other secondary figures given: **10.1–11.4 psi** and **10.0–11.0 psi (0.69–0.76 bar)** — [padelearnings [weak]](https://padelearnings.com/blog/best-padel-balls). **Recommendation:** quote the FIP wording exactly, or say "lower pressure than a tennis ball" without a number.
- **Altitude ball:** the rules allow another type of ball with a bounce of **more than 121.92 cm and less than 135 cm** for high altitude. The 2026 rules set the threshold at **1,000 m** above sea level; the older FIP ball-approval document set it at **800 m** — [FIP Rules](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/FIP-Rules-of-Padel.pdf); [FIP Padel Balls Approval](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/PADEL-BALLS-APPROVAL-2024-1.pdf)
- **FIP-approved ball lists** exist at least from the list "Effective: 1st of January **2020**" — [FIP Approved Padel Balls 2020](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/FIP-Approved-Padel-Balls-2020.pdf); 2025 list: [FIP Certifications Approved Balls 2025](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/PADEL-BALLS-APPROVAL-2025-6.pdf)
- **Ball colour, 2026 (conflict, both weak):** one blog says **white or yellow only**; another says **any colour** is now allowed — [padelbarcelona.es [weak]](https://padelbarcelona.es/padel/nuevas-normas-padel-2026-reglamento-fip-guia-completa/); [pala-hack.com [weak]](https://pala-hack.com/blog/cambios-normativa-padel-2026/)
- **Tennis comparison (ITF):**
  - Mass **56.0–59.4 g** — [ITF ball approval tests](https://m.itftennis.com/media/2100/balls-ball-approval-tests.pdf)
  - Rebound from a **100 in (254 cm)** drop of about **134.6–147.3 cm** (≈135–147 cm) — [older ITF rules text (govdocs)](https://corpora.tika.apache.org/base/docs/govdocs1/124/124247.pdf); [2018 ITF Rules of Tennis](https://hts.hr/wp-content/uploads/__2019/07/2018_ITF_Rules_of_Tennis.pdf)
  - Diameter **6.54–6.86 cm**, seen only in a weak source quoting Appendix I — [sportssurge.alibaba [weak]](https://sportssurge.alibaba.com/tennis/what-is-the-diameter-of-tennis-ball)
  - Tennis pressure **about 12–14 psi**; the ITF figure of 14 psi is quoted when the can's seal is broken — [padelradar.io [weak]](https://padelradar.io/en/blog/padelbaelle-ratgeber); [padel-wien.at [weak]](https://www.padel-wien.at/en/padel-ball-vs-tennis-ball/)
- **Effect:** the lower pressure makes the padel ball bounce lower and fly slower, so it stays playable off the glass — [padelradar.io [weak]](https://padelradar.io/es/blog/padelbaelle-ratgeber)

### Inferences
- The weight bands are identical. The padel ball's diameter band (6.35–6.77) overlaps but sits below tennis's (6.54–6.86), and its maximum bounce is 2 cm lower (145 vs 147 cm). The honest on-screen line is: same weight, slightly smaller, slightly less bouncy, lower pressure.
- Padel inherited tennis balls (Corcuera kept the tennis net and scoring), so early play almost certainly used tennis balls. No source states this, so it must not be narrated as fact.

### Gaps

- **The ITF diameter** needs checking against the current ITF Rules of Tennis Appendix I. Only a weak source carried it.
- **The pressure unit**: the FIP text could not be opened to check its exact wording.

## 4. Rules and scoring: serve, walls, playing outside, golden point, Star Point, and the 2026 edition

### Takeaway
The core rules have survived from the start:
- an underhand serve, bounced first and struck at or below the waist
- diagonal service boxes
- the ball may be played off your own walls but must not hit the opponents' walls or fence before bouncing
- tennis scoring

The modern changes are mostly at deuce:
- **WPT golden point**, mandatory from the 2020 season (debut at the Marbella Master 2020)
- **Premier Padel** dropped the golden point and played advantage from its first season (Doha, March 2022)
- **Star Point**: approved unanimously by the FIP General Assembly on 28 Nov 2025, announced 18 Dec 2025, in force 1 Jan 2026. It debuted on the CUPRA FIP Tour in Melbourne in January 2026 and on Premier Padel at the Riyadh P1, 9–14 Feb 2026.

The 2026 FIP edition also relaxed the serve stance to one foot behind the line, where both feet were required before.

### Cited Findings
**Serve**
- The ball must bounce in the correct (diagonal) service box. At contact it must be **at or below waist level**, with **at least one foot on the ground** — [FIP Rules of Padel 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf)
- **2026 change:** the server must have **one foot behind the service line**, between the imaginary extension of the centre line and the side wall, until the ball is served. **The 2023 rulebook required both feet** — [FIP Rules 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf) vs [FIP game regulations 2023](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2-game-regulations.pdf). Caveat: the 2024-uploaded FIP file headed "In force as of 1.01.2026" also has the one-foot wording, so the change may have been published as early as late 2024 — [FIP 2024 upload](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/FIP-Rules-of-Padel.pdf)
- A blog quotes Rule 6.2 (2026): "The ball may not cross the service line or the imaginary line until it is struck." Spanish blogs say the 2026 text explicitly forbids striking the ball inside the projection of the service box or to the side — [Pelotista [weak]](https://pelotista.com/blog/padel-rules); [padelnuestro [weak/shop]](https://www.padelnuestro.com/blog/nuevas-reglas-padel-2026); [padeladdict](https://www.padeladdict.com/nuevas-normas-padel-2026-reglamento-fip/)
- **Serve fault:** the ball bounces in the receiver's box and then touches the **metallic fence** before its second bounce — [FIP Rules](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf)
- **Let ("net"):** a serve that touches the net or posts, lands in the correct box and does **not** touch the fence before the second bounce is replayed — [FIP Rules](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf); [LTA Padel rules](https://www.ltapadel.org.uk/play/how-to-get-started-playing-padel/rules/)

**Walls and fence in the rally**
- A player may return the ball directly or **off the walls of their own court**. A player loses the point if the ball they hit, **without bouncing first, hits the opponents' walls, metallic fence** or any external element on the opponents' side — [FIP Rules 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf)
- In an older FIP edition, a player also loses the point if their shot touches **the metallic fence or the ground on their own side** — [FIP game regulations 2023](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2-game-regulations.pdf)
- A player loses the point if they, their racket or anything they wear or carry touches the **net, posts, tension cable** or any part of the opponents' court while the ball is in play — [FIP Rules](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf)
- A returned ball that clips the net stays in play if it lands correctly in the opponents' court — [LTA Padel](https://www.ltapadel.org.uk/play/how-to-get-started-playing-padel/rules/)

**Playing outside the court ("por tres" / "por cuatro")**
- The FIP rulebook has a **Rule 16, "Juego autorizado fuera de la pista"** (play authorised outside the court). It appears in the **2021** revision and the **2026** revision — [FIP Reglamento de Juego](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/2-Reglamento-Juego.pdf); [FIP Reglas del Pádel 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Reglas-del-Padel.pdf)
- Out-of-court play is **not automatic**. It must be authorised before the match, and the court must meet the safety-zone requirements given in section 1 — [padelstar.es [weak]](https://padelstar.es/reglas-de-padel/reglamento-juego-autorizado-fuera-de-la-pista/); [FIP Rules 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf)
- When the ball leaves **over the back** wall, play can continue. If it leaves **over the side wall or through the door**, the point is lost when it bounces a second time or touches an element outside the court (a blog's reading of the FIP rule). Where out-of-court play is authorised, players may run out and play it back — [padelstar.es [weak]](https://padelstar.es/reglas-de-padel/reglamento-juego-autorizado-fuera-de-la-pista/)
- **"Por 3":** a smash that, after bouncing, is driven into the back wall and leaves over the side **more than 3 m** up. **"Por 4":** the same shot leaving at **more than 4 m** — [Totalpadel vocabulary [weak]](https://www.totalpadel.com/lc/culture/vocabulary-of-padel.html?lang=es). A shop glossary gives a different reading: "por tres" leaves over the **side** wall, "por cuatro" over the **back** wall — [tiendapadelpoint [weak/shop]](https://www.tiendapadelpoint.com/faqs-remate-por-tres-padel). Academic papers use "remate por tres" as a technical term — [Análisis espacio-temporal… remate por tres (SciELO)](https://www.sidalc.net/search/Record/oai:scielo:S1888-75462015000400031)

**The golden point (punto de oro)**
- **The World Padel Tour adopted the golden point from the 2020 season**: one deciding point at 40–40, with the receiving pair choosing to receive on the right or the left — [University of Extremadura, e-balonmano.com study](https://revista-ebalonmano.unex.es/index.php/ebalonmano/article/download/2089/1966); [Universidad de Málaga, RICCAFD](https://revistas.uma.es/index.php/riccafd/article/download/16499/17695/85203)
- It debuted at the **Cervezas Victoria Marbella Master 2020**. It applied to men and women from the first event of the 2020 season and was mandatory in every match. WPT director-general **Mario Hernando** presented it as a way to make the game more dynamic and attractive — [Padel Addict](https://www.padeladdict.com/punto-de-oro-world-padel-tour/)
- **Measured effect:** academic studies found more games per set and significantly more breaks of serve after the golden point came in (WPT 2019 vs 2020, and Marbella 2019 vs 2020) — [UNEX study](https://dehesa.unex.es/handle/10662/17269); [UCAM, CCD](https://ccd.ucam.edu/visores/18_56/1966-en/index.html)
- **Premier Padel did not use it.** Its first event (Doha, **March 2022**) was played with **advantage** ("finally the golden point is gone"). A later report says Premier Padel never played golden point, and kept advantage after it took over the world tour — [Padel Alto, 30 Mar 2022 [blog, first-hand]](https://padelalto.com/2022/03/30/day-2-from-doha-finally-the-golden-point-is-gone-and-all-three-relatives-involved-in-todays-surprises); [Padel Alto, 26 Dec 2023 [blog]](https://padelalto.com/2023/12/26/details-premier-padel-has-taken-a-decision-about-golden-point)

**The Star Point (2026)**
- **Approval:** on **28 November 2025** the FIP General Assembly, made up of **100 national federations**, **unanimously** approved an update to the FIP *Rules of Padel* adding a new scoring format — [FIP, "Between innovation and tradition: introducing the Star Point"](https://www.padelfip.com/2025/12/between-innovation-and-tradition-introducing-the-star-point-the-scoring-system-that-appeals-to-everyone/); [FIP press release 18 Dec 2025](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/18-december_Star-Point_ENG.pdf)
- **Announced 18 December 2025**, alongside the 2026 Qatar Airways Premier Padel calendar — [Premier Padel](https://premierpadel.com/en/news/premier-padel-and-international-padel-federation-unveil-2026-qatar-airways-premier-padel-tour-calendar-and-innovative-new-star-point-system-launched); [FIP](https://www.padelfip.com/2025/12/premier-padel-and-international-padel-federation-unveil-2026-qatar-airways-premier-padel-tour-calendar-and-innovative-new-star-point-system-launched/)
- **How it works (FIP rules text, effective 01.01.2026, "Option 2: Star Point"):**
  1. At deuce, the next point won gives **"advantage 1"**. Winning the following point wins the game; losing it returns the score to **"deuce 2"**.
  2. The same happens at **"advantage 2"**: winning the next point wins the game, losing it gives **"deuce 3"**.
  3. At deuce 3 a single deciding point, the **"Star Point"**, is played.
  4. The **receiving pair chooses the side** to receive from, and its players **may not change positions**. In mixed matches the receiver must be the same sex as the server.
  5. The pair that wins the Star Point wins the game.
  - Source: [FIP Rules of Padel 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf). The Spanish text ("Método 2") matches, and adds that the Star Point may carry a sponsor's name — [FIP Reglas del Pádel 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Reglas-del-Padel-1.pdf)
  - Summary sequence: deuce 1 → advantage 1 → deuce 2 → advantage 2 → deuce 3 → Star Point. A game therefore has at most two advantage cycles before sudden death.
- **Three options in the 2026 rules:** "Option 1: Advantage" (traditional), "Option 2: Star Point", "Option 3: Golden Point" (sudden death at the first deuce, receivers choose side) — [FIP Rules of Padel 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf). One search extract garbled the option numbering; two others agreed on the numbering above.
- **Where it applies in 2026:** **Premier Padel, CUPRA FIP Tour, FIP Promises (juniors) and the amateur FIP Beyond**. FIP Promises rules make it mandatory for all matches on the FIP Promises World and Continental Tours — [FIP](https://www.padelfip.com/2025/12/between-innovation-and-tradition-introducing-the-star-point-the-scoring-system-that-appeals-to-everyone/); [FIP Promises rulebook, Jan 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/EN-FIP-Promises-Tour-Rulebook-09_01_2026.pdf)
- **Debuts:** the **CUPRA FIP Tour at the Melbourne stop** in January 2026 (a secondary source dates the FIP Bronze Melbourne to **5 Jan 2026**). **Premier Padel at the Riyadh Season P1, 9–14 February 2026**, agreed between Premier Padel and the FIP "after consultations" — [FIP](https://www.padelfip.com/2025/12/premier-padel-and-international-padel-federation-unveil-2026-qatar-airways-premier-padel-tour-calendar-and-innovative-new-star-point-system-launched/); [Premier Padel](https://premierpadel.com/en/news/premier-padel-and-international-padel-federation-unveil-2026-qatar-airways-premier-padel-tour-calendar-and-innovative-new-star-point-system-launched); [thepadelbrief [weak]](https://thepadelbrief.com/en/blog/padel-star-point-system-explained)
- **Aim:** to limit the maximum number of points in a game and avoid prolonged advantage play — [FIP](https://www.padelfip.com/2025/12/between-innovation-and-tradition-introducing-the-star-point-the-scoring-system-that-appeals-to-everyone/). A secondary source says testing cut the average game from **4.5 to 2.75 minutes** — [padelnuestro [weak/shop]](https://www.padelnuestro.com/int/blog/star-point-padel). This is unverified and not in the FIP extracts.
- **Player reaction:** described as **divided** after the first months of 2026. A claimed figure that attacking teams win about **62%** of Star Points could not be verified — [snelpadel.nl [weak]](https://snelpadel.nl/star-point-padel/)
- **Not universal:** organisers of other tournaments may still choose Advantage or Golden Point. The 2026 rules keep all three options — [thepadelbrief [weak]](https://thepadelbrief.com/en/blog/new-padel-rules-2026); [FIP Rules 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf)

**Other reported 2026 changes (mostly weak sources; verify before narrating)**

## 5. Who writes the rules, and notable editions

### Takeaway
The International Padel Federation (FIP) writes the *Rules of Padel* (*Reglamento de Juego* / *Reglas del Pádel*). It was founded in Madrid on 12 July 1991 by the Argentine, Spanish and Uruguayan associations. National federations, such as Spain's FEP, publish their own reglamento based on it. Editions found: 2017, 2021, 2023/2024 uploads, and the edition "Review of application 01.01.2026", which added the Star Point and the one-foot serve.

### Cited Findings
- **FIP founded:** in **Madrid on 12 July 1991** by the padel associations of **Argentina, Spain and Uruguay**. The founding meeting is reported at the **Club de Golf La Moraleja**, and **Julio Alegría Artiach** was the first president — [FIP History](https://www.padelfip.com/history/); [FIP By-Laws 2021](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/1-ByLaws_FIP_Eng_2021.pdf); [FIP 2024 feature](https://www.padelfip.com/2024/10/from-argentina-and-spain-to-global-fame-the-padel-passion-that-conquered-the-world/)
- **First World Championships:** **1992**, held in Madrid and Seville, with the finals at **Expo 92** — [FIP History](https://www.padelfip.com/history/)
- **Spain:** the Asociación Española de Padel was founded in **1991**, registered under the acronym "P.A.D.E.L.". Spain's Consejo Superior de Deportes recognised padel as a sport in **1993** — [FIP History / FIP 2024 feature](https://www.padelfip.com/history/)
- **Membership:** **100 national federations** voted on the Star Point in Nov 2025. The **36th FIP General Assembly** (Madrid, Oct 2026) reported **115 member federations** — [FIP, Oct 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/2026/10/fip-general-assembly-strengthens-global-unity-as-membership-reaches-115-federations/)
- **Editions located:**
  - **FIP 2017** (Revision 01.01.2017) — [hosted by the FEP](https://www.padelfederacion.es/refs/docs/REGLAMENTO_DE_JUEGO_FIP_2017.pdf)
  - **2021 revision** (contains Rule 16 on playing outside the court) — [FIP Reglamento de Juego](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/2-Reglamento-Juego.pdf)
  - **2023 upload** (both feet behind the line; EN 12150-1 glass) — [FIP game regulations](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2-game-regulations.pdf)
  - **2024 upload** titled "In force as of 1.01.2026" — [FIP](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/FIP-Rules-of-Padel.pdf)
  - **December 2025 upload** "Review of application 01.01.2026", which includes the Star Point — [EN](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf); [ES](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Reglas-del-Padel.pdf)
- **Spanish federation reglamento:** [FEP Reglamento de Juego](https://www.padelfederacion.es/refs/docs/REGLAMENTO_JUEGO_FEP.pdf)
- **Pro tours have their own rulebooks on top of the FIP rules:** e.g. [Premier Padel Rulebook Men's 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Premier-Padel-Rulebook-Men%C2%B4s_EN-1-1.pdf); [CUPRA FIP Tour Rulebook 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Cupra-FIP-Tour-Rulebook_EN-1.pdf). The **LTA** (UK) publishes a referee guide for 2026 — [LTA Padel Referee and Organiser Guide 2026](https://www.lta.org.uk/4a76eb/siteassets/roles/officials/files/lta-padel-referee-and-organiser-guide-2026.pdf)
