## 1. Enrique Corcuera in Acapulco: what he built, when, why, how big, from what, under what name, and who he and Viviana were

### Takeaway
The best-supported account is this. In 1969 (La Nación says October 1969), Enrique Corcuera García Pimentel built an enclosed court of about 20 × 10 m at his house in Las Brisas, Acapulco. It had 3 m walls at the ends and wire mesh or fencing, with a tennis net across the middle. He called the game "Paddle Corcuera".

The year is disputed. His widow says 1968, a legend repeated by Spanish Wikipedia and La Nación says 1962, and his son traces the idea to a frontón on the family's Jalisco hacienda (Estipac) in the 1950s. The motive is also told several ways: no room for a full tennis court on a sloping lot, jungle vegetation, chasing lost balls, or a disturbed siesta.

### Inferences
- The safest narration: "In 1969 — the date most federations and newspapers give — Enrique Corcuera built a walled court at his house in Las Brisas, Acapulco." Then note that his widow remembers 1968 and his son traces the idea to a frontón on the family hacienda in Jalisco in the 1950s.
- The 20 × 10 m figure is the modern standard. Sources may be projecting it back onto 1969: Nallé Grinda recalls Acapulco courts of 18–20 m (section 5). The 20 × 10 claim for the first court rests on FIP and La Nación, not on a measured plan.
- The motive versions can be reconciled: a steep lot without room for a full tennis court, vegetation, and walls added so nobody had to fetch balls. They are compatible but come from different tellers. The siesta story and "Viviana wanted tennis lessons" are anecdotes from the family or its circle.
- "Corcuera García Pimentel" (La Nación) and "Corcuera García y Pimentel" (Spanish Wikipedia) are the same name in two forms.

## 2. Precursors and the name: paddle tennis, platform tennis, "padder tennis", ship-deck claims, and the word "pádel"

### Takeaway
Padel descends most directly from two American games:

- **Paddle tennis**, invented by Frank Peer Beal: a half-size tennis court with short wooden paddles. Beal developed it as a boy in Albion, Michigan (dated 1898). Courts were laid in Washington Square Park, New York in 1915, and the first tournament followed in 1922.
- **Platform tennis**, from Scarsdale, NY in 1928: paddles on a raised wooden platform surrounded by chicken-wire screens, with play off the screens allowed after the bounce.

Padel takes the paddle and the small court from the first, and the idea of playing the ball off an enclosure from the second, but uses solid walls. "Padder tennis" is a British/New Zealand name for a paddle-tennis variant (a 1951 Pathé newsreel), not a ship-deck ancestor. No source found documents the British cruise-ship origin story. The word "pádel" is the Spanish adaptation of English "paddle (tennis)", per the RAE.

### Cited Findings
**Paddle tennis**
- Paddle tennis was invented by Episcopal minister Frank Peer Beal in Albion, Michigan, in 1898. The original court was 18 ft × 39 ft, exactly half a regulation tennis court. In 1915 Beal had the Manhattan parks department lay courts in Washington Square Park. The first tournament was in 1922 and the US Paddle Tennis Association formed in 1923. Murray Geller, a player of the 1940s–50s, shaped the modern game. — [Wikipedia (en), Paddle tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddle_tennis) [WIKI]
- Britannica says Beal invented it as a child in Albion and, as a New York City official, introduced it on NYC playgrounds in the early 1920s. — [Britannica, Frank P. Beal](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Frank-P-Beal) [reputable reference]
- A POP Tennis history says Beal was transferred to New York City in 1921 and the first tournament followed the next year. — [NC POP Tennis, History](http://ncpoptennis.com/history-of-pop-tennis) [WEAK]

**Platform tennis**
- In fall 1928 neighbours Fessenden Blanchard and James Cogswell looked for an outdoor winter sport. They built a wooden platform, 48 ft × 20 ft, on Cogswell's land in Scarsdale, NY, finished by the end of November 1928. The Hall of Fame describes it as first a deck for deck tennis and badminton. By the end of 1932 Scarsdale had seven platforms and the first open tournament was held, won by Earle Gatchell and Fessenden Blanchard. — [Platform Tennis Hall of Fame, History of the Game](https://platformtennishalloffame.org/history-of-the-game/) [OFFICIAL, sport's hall of fame]
- They got paddles and sponge-rubber balls from a local store, which was paddle-tennis equipment. Tired of digging balls out of snowbanks, they put up chicken-wire fencing. They then agreed that a ball could be played off the screens after it had bounced in the court. The first screens were two-inch-mesh chicken wire stapled to 8 ft two-by-fours, rising to 12 ft by 1932. Donald Evans's 1934 design stretched one-inch mesh inside the uprights. Frank Contessa made early plywood paddles. — [Platform Tennis Hall of Fame](https://platformtennishalloffame.org/history-of-the-game/); [Hall of Fame, Frank B. Contessa](https://platformtennishalloffame.org/frank-b-contessa-the-missing-link-between-paddle-and-platform-tennis/); [APTA (old site), What is Platform Tennis](https://old.platformtennis.org/about_us/What-is-Platform-Tennis.htm) [OFFICIAL]
- Britannica calls platform tennis a combination of tennis and squash, devised in 1928 by Blanchard and Cogswell at Scarsdale. — [Britannica, Platform tennis](https://www.britannica.com/sports/platform-tennis)
- Fox Meadow Tennis Club (Scarsdale) became the first platform-tennis club. Sources differ on its first court: 1931, or three years after 1928. — [Hall of Fame, Fox Meadow 80 years](https://platformtennishalloffame.org/event/fox-meadow-80-years-of-platform-tennis/); [Fox Meadow Tennis Club](http://fmtcfmtc.com/)

### Inferences
- Padel and paddle tennis share the solid short bat and the small court. Padel and platform tennis share an enclosure the ball may be played off after the bounce. What is new in padel is solid walls (concrete or cement) at the ends, in doubles, on a court about 20 × 10 m. Platform tennis uses wire screens on a 44 × 20 ft playing area within a raised deck.
- The "game called padel" Viviana says Enrique played without walls in England and France may have been paddle tennis or a British variant such as padder tennis. That is speculation; no source says so.
- The 19th-century "British ships" origin should not be narrated as fact. It has no primary support in anything found, and seems to be confused with deck tennis or paddle tennis. The Hall of Fame notes the first Scarsdale platform was built for deck tennis.

### Gaps
- Could not read the USPTA's or APTA's current official history pages directly. The APTA material came from an older APTA page and the Hall of Fame.
- No etymology was found for the British coinage "padder", and no date for padder tennis earlier than the 1951 Pathé film.
- The exact year "pádel" entered the DLE was not confirmed (Echegaray reportedly says 2014; unverified).

## 3. Alfonso de Hohenlohe, the Marbella Club, Manolo Santana, and the first Spanish courts, clubs and tournaments

### Takeaway
Prince Alfonso of Hohenlohe-Langenburg (Madrid 1924 – Marbella 2003) founded the Marbella Club in 1954. He learned the game at Corcuera's house in Acapulco. In **1974** he had the Marbella Club's tennis court converted: net taken out, cement walls built at both ends, wire mesh down the sides. This is the best-supported "first court in Spain/Europe", backed by the hotel's own history, which cites Nicholas Foulkes's anniversary book, and by Wikipedia.

FIP's own wording is vaguer ("early 1970s"), and some blogs say 1970. The claim that Hohenlohe "changed the dimensions and rules" is widely repeated, but no source specifies the changes. Manolo Santana's early role (organising tournaments on the Costa del Sol) appears only on weak sources. His documented Marbella club roles start in 1983.

### Cited Findings
**Hohenlohe and the Marbella Club**
- Alfonso de Hohenlohe-Langenburg was born in Madrid on 28 May 1924 and died in Marbella on 21 December 2003. He was a German-Spanish noble and property developer, founder of the Marbella Club Hotel. — [Wikipedia (es), Alfonso de Hohenlohe-Langenburg](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_de_Hohenlohe-Langenburg) [WIKI]
- He married Ira von Fürstenberg in 1955, and later sold his shares in the Marbella Club. — [Wikipedia (en), Prince Alfonso of Hohenlohe-Langenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Alfonso_of_Hohenlohe-Langenburg); [Wikipedia (en), Ira von Fürstenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ira_von_F%C3%BCrstenberg) [WIKI]
- The Marbella Club was "born" in 1954 from the von Hohenlohe family's Mediterranean hideaway, Finca Santa Margarita. — [Marbella Club, Our Story](https://www.marbellaclub.com/our-story); [Marbella Club, 70 years](https://www.marbellaclub.com/media-centre/the-evolution-of-an-icon-marbella-club-celebrates-70-years) [OFFICIAL]

**The 1974 Marbella Club court**
- The Marbella Club's anniversary trivia excerpts **Nicholas Foulkes**'s 60th-anniversary book. In it, workers arrived at the club's tennis court one day in 1974, "uprooted the net and erected cement walls at either end", with chicken-wire-style mesh along the sides. Alfonso called it a new game, "padel tennis".
- The same page says his Mexican friend Enrique Corcuera had built a court at his house, "inventing a game played with wooden paddles and tennis balls with rules like those of squash", and later published "The Corcuera Rules".
- The same page says the original courts disappeared in 1995 in one of the club's expansions; "all that remains is a bronze plaque embedded in a large rock".
- Source for the three points above: [Marbella Club, Anniversary Trivia](https://www.marbellaclub.com/blog/marbella-club-anniversary-trivia) [OFFICIAL, citing a book]
- The hotel's FAQ now calls the Marbella Club "home to the first padel court ever built in Europe — a historic landmark near our Finca Ana María". Its Finca Ana María page speaks of reviving "the original padel court". Its padel page presents a "Prince Alfonso" court as a homage to the 1970s. — [Marbella Club FAQ](https://www.marbellaclub.com/faq); [Marbella Club, Finca Ana María](https://www.marbellaclub.com/finca-ana-maria); [Marbella Club, Padel](https://www.marbellaclub.com/explore/paddle) [OFFICIAL, marketing]. This conflicts with the trivia page.
- Vallparc reports a plaque at the resort reading "Aquí nació el pádel tenis en 1974". It also says Hohenlohe's courts swapped the side walls for fencing ("rejas") and slightly modified the original rules. — [Vallparc](https://www.vallparc.com/la-amistad-hispano-mexicana-que-marco-el-origen-del-padel-en-espana/) [WEAK, club blog]

**How and when Hohenlohe learned the game**
- English Wikipedia says Hohenlohe learned of padel visiting Corcuera in Acapulco and, back in Spain, built the first two courts at the Marbella Club Hotel in 1974. Given the resort's exclusivity, the game was at first a jet-set pastime. — [Wikipedia (en), Padel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padel) [WIKI]
- Spanish Wikipedia says that in 1974, on a visit to Corcuera's house, he was enchanted by the game. Back in Marbella he made "some modifications to the court and the rules", creating Spain's first two courts. — [Wikipedia (es), Alfonso de Hohenlohe-Langenburg](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_de_Hohenlohe-Langenburg) [WIKI]
- FIP says Hohenlohe "spent his summer holidays at Corcuera's home in Acapulco" and dates Spain's courts only to the "early 1970s". — [FIP, History](https://www.padelfip.com/history/); [FIP, "From Argentina and Spain to global fame" (Oct 2024)](https://www.padelfip.com/2024/10/from-argentina-and-spain-to-global-fame-the-padel-passion-that-conquered-the-world/) [OFFICIAL]
- LTA Padel says he visited Mexico in 1974 and imported the game to the Costa del Sol. — [LTA Padel](https://www.ltapadel.org.uk/what-we-do/what-is-the-history-of-padel/) [OFFICIAL]
- Padel Magazine says Hohenlohe discovered the game at Las Brisas and built the first courts at the Marbella Club "around 1974". — [Padel Magazine fact-check](https://padel-magazine.co.uk/The-program-%22Watch-your-step%22-was-mistaken-about-the-origin-of-the-padel--we-checked/) [SPECIALIST]
- Some sources date the Marbella courts to 1970. — [iPadel UK](https://ipadel.co.uk/History-of-Padel-Tennis); [padelstar.es](http://padelstar.es/historia-del-padel/historia-del-padel/) [WEAK]. A reader comment on padelstar disputes 1970.
- Another weak source places the first Spanish courts at the "Marbella Tennis Club". — [Vacation Marbella](https://vacationmarbella.com/inspiration/padel-marbella/) [WEAK]

**What Hohenlohe changed**
- Blogs say he introduced changes "to make it more competitive", and that early Spanish courts did not follow official measurements (some shorter, some wider) until standardisation in the 1980s. No specific dimension or rule change is named. — [padelrenting](https://www.padelrenting.com/blog/noticias-1/la-primera-pista-de-padel-en-espana-23); [Betway blog](https://blog.betway.es/padel/padel-en-espana-una-historia-de-amor-a-primera-vista/) [WEAK]

**Manolo Santana, Puente Romano and the first Spanish clubs**
- Manolo Santana was drawn in by Hohenlohe, organised many padel tournaments and promoted the game along the Costa del Sol, leading clubs to build courts. — [Betway blog](https://blog.betway.es/padel/padel-en-espana-una-historia-de-amor-a-primera-vista/); [padeladdict](https://www.padeladdict.com/historia-del-padel/); [planetapadel](https://www.planetapadel.com/2019/06/descubre-cual-fue-la-primera-pista-de.html) [WEAK]. No named tournament or year found.
- Puente Romano's tennis club opened in 1979, first run by Björn Borg. Manolo Santana took it over in 1983 and ran it until 1998. — [Wikipedia (en), Club de Tenis Puente Romano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_de_Tenis_Puente_Romano) [WIKI]
- The Manolo Santana Racquets Club in Marbella dates from 1996. — [padeldir](https://padeldir.com/en/spain/marbella/manolo-santana-racquets-club/) [WEAK]
- HEAD Padel has issued a limited "Alfonso de Hohenlohe" collection framed around padel's history. — [HEAD, "Alfonso de Hohenlohe and the History of Padel"](https://www.head.com/en_US/rs/stories/hohenlohe-history); [PadelSpain](https://www.padelspain.net/material/22301/Nueva-coleccion-inspirada-en-la-historia-del-padel-Alfonso-de-Hohenlohe-by-HEAD-Padel/) [commercial]

### Inferences
- The narration can say "In 1974, at the Marbella Club…" with good support: the hotel's own history citing Foulkes, LTA, and both Wikipedias. FIP's "early 1970s" is not a contradiction, just less precise. The 1970 date is weakly sourced and should be dropped or mentioned only as a variant.
- The best-documented physical change at Marbella is open wire mesh on the sides, with cement walls only at the ends; "rejas" according to Vallparc. Accounts of Corcuera's court are mixed (walls at the ends plus mesh, versus walls all round). Most sources put concrete ends and mesh on the sides in Spain. "Hohenlohe changed the dimensions" is unsupported in what was found.
- Santana's role before 1980 is not documented by any reputable source found. Narrate it, if at all, as "later" or "is credited with", or leave it out.

### Gaps
- No contemporary (1974–80) Spanish press account of the Marbella courts was found; ABC and El País archives were blocked.
- I found no first Spanish tournament before 1980, by name or date.
- I could not confirm whether there were two courts or one in 1974. Wikipedia and FIP say two; Foulkes's account describes a single tennis court converted.
- I could not see the Foulkes book itself. Its full title is not confirmed from the extract.

## 4. Arrival in Argentina (Menditeguy, Mar del Plata) and Uruguay

### Takeaway
An Argentine Menditeguy brought the game to Argentina in the early-to-mid 1970s. Most official sources name **Julio Menditeguy**; La Capital (Mar del Plata) names his brother **Carlos "Charly" Menditeguy**. LTA says 1975; La Nación and FIP say "early 1970s"/"around the same time". The route is also disputed: FIP says via Hohenlohe's Marbella club, while Viviana Corcuera says Julio visited Acapulco and "took the measurements".

The first Argentine courts are named as the Menditeguy family's stud farm (haras) in San Antonio de Areco and the Blaquier family's estancia La Biznaga. Then came Club Tortugas and the Ocean Club in Mar del Plata. The boom came in the 1980s. Uruguay's documented history found here starts in the 1980s (Punta del Este, 1983).

### Cited Findings
**Who brought it, and by which route**
- FIP says that around the same time as Spain, Julio Menditeguy, "an Argentine member of Alfonso's tennis club in Marbella", brought the sport to Argentina. Early courts followed at Club Tortugas, the Mar del Plata Ocean Club and other cities. — [FIP, History](https://www.padelfip.com/history/); [FIP, "From Argentina and Spain to global fame"](https://www.padelfip.com/2024/10/from-argentina-and-spain-to-global-fame-the-padel-passion-that-conquered-the-world/) [OFFICIAL]
- LTA Padel dates Argentina's arrival to 1975. — [LTA Padel](https://www.ltapadel.org.uk/what-we-do/what-is-the-history-of-padel/) [OFFICIAL]
- Viviana Corcuera, quoted by La Nación, says Julio Menditeguy was among the Argentines who visited the Corcueras in Acapulco: "Él se llevó las medidas y características de la cancha y la hizo en la Argentina. Fue el primero." La Nación also calls him a childhood friend of Corcuera. — [La Nación (2026)](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/lifestyle/enrique-y-viviana-corcuera-el-magnate-mexicano-que-invento-el-padel-y-la-miss-argentina-que-escribio-nid01102026/) [PRESS quoting FAMILY]
- Viviana separately says Argentine tennis players visited "around 1970" and were thrilled playing at her house. — [Minter Dial (2025)](https://www.minterdial.com/2025/05/rackets-walls-and-passion-viviana-corcuera-reveals-the-origins-of-padel-audio-in-spanish/) [FAMILY]
- Vallparc says many Argentine polo players who frequented the Marbella Club swapped horses for padel, and Julio Menditeguy imported the game. — [Vallparc](https://www.vallparc.com/la-amistad-hispano-mexicana-que-marco-el-origen-del-padel-en-espana/) [WEAK]

**First Argentine courts**
- In the early 1970s the first two Argentine courts were built at the Menditeguy family haras in San Antonio de Areco and at the Blaquier family's estancia La Biznaga, followed by a court at the Ocean Club in Mar del Plata. — [La Nación, "El extraño invento de un millonario"](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/deportes/el-extrano-invento-de-un-millonario-nid465796/) [PRESS]
- Blogs name Club Tortugas, the Mar del Plata Ocean Club and the family's Haras Vacación and El Turf, and say Menditeguy built Argentina's first court in 1975. — [iPadel UK](https://ipadel.co.uk/History-of-Padel-Tennis); [Sensa Padel](https://sensapadel.com/the-global-journey-of-padel-from-mexico-to-the-world/) [WEAK]

**Julio or Charly Menditeguy**
- La Capital (Mar del Plata) attributes the start to **Carlos "Charly" Menditeguy**, who learned of it through Corcuera, "a Mexican businessman who built a wall that acted as another frontón and put a net in the middle". It says the game reached Mar del Plata through the Ocean Club as an entertainment. — [La Capital MdP, "Historia de la evolución del paddle en Argentina"](https://www.lacapitalmdp.com/historia-de-la-evolucion-del-paddle-en-argentina/) [PRESS, local]
- La Prensa names Charly Menditeguy as one of padel's promoters in Argentina. — [La Prensa, "Charly Menditeguy, un personaje del deporte"](https://www.laprensa.com.ar/Charly-Menditeguy-un-personaje-del-deporte-536017.note.aspx) [PRESS]
- Infobae says that after polo and motor racing, Charlie and his brother Julio turned to thoroughbreds with Haras El Turf. — [Infobae (Apr 2023)](https://www.infobae.com/historias/2023/04/27/charlie-menditeguy-el-millonario-playboy-que-fue-crack-en-todos-los-deportes-y-conquisto-a-brigitte-bardot/) [PRESS]
- Find a Grave lists **Julio Menditeguy (1914–2003)** as a breeder of thoroughbred racehorses. — [Find a Grave](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/25352475/julio-menditeguy) [WEAK, user-contributed]
- The surname also appears as "Menditengui" on some sites. — per [search summary of padelfans/feppadel pages](https://padelfans.co.uk/learn/who-invented-padel/) [WEAK]

**Later growth in Argentina (for context, mostly after 1980)**
- In 1987 Argentina had 330 courts, 220 of them in Mar del Plata. The Asociación Pádel Argentino was founded in 1988, and the first international match was played in Mar del Plata that year. — [La Nación (via search extract)](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/lifestyle/enrique-y-viviana-corcuera-el-magnate-mexicano-que-invento-el-padel-y-la-miss-argentina-que-escribio-nid01102026/) [PRESS]. The extract did not say which La Nación article carries these numbers.
- In Mar del Plata, the Ocean Club built the city's first courts. The game "took off" there only in the 1980s, with La Barraca, La Mirage and Forum. La Capital says Forum hosted "the first World Championship" in 1987. — [La Capital MdP](https://www.lacapitalmdp.com/historia-de-la-evolucion-del-paddle-en-argentina/) [PRESS, local]
- FIP gives 1988 for the Argentine Padel Association. — [FIP](https://www.padelfip.com/2024/10/from-argentina-and-spain-to-global-fame-the-padel-passion-that-conquered-the-world/)

**Uruguay**
- In summer 1983 a court went up at Balneario Zorba (Punta del Este), drawing holidaymakers with Argentine tennis figures Ricardo Cano and Roberto "Cacho" Aubone. In the mid-1990s the property boom replaced padel sheds with buildings, and concrete courts caused injuries. In 2006 the Ministry of Tourism gave the body federative status; it is today the Asociación de Pádel del Uruguay. — [Búsqueda](https://www.busqueda.com.uy/tiempo-libre/el-ave-fenix-los-deportes-el-padel-regreso-y-mas-fuerza-que-nunca-n5393642); [El Observador](https://www.elobservador.com.uy/nota/la-vuelta-del-padel-las-claves-que-explican-el-furor-por-el-deporte-insignia-de-los-90--20237305010) [PRESS]. The extract did not say which of the two carries the 1983 date.
- FIP was founded in Madrid in July 1991 by representatives of the Argentine, Spanish and Uruguayan bodies. — [Wikipedia (en), International Padel Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Padel_Federation); [FIP](https://www.padelfip.com/2024/10/from-argentina-and-spain-to-global-fame-the-padel-passion-that-conquered-the-world/)

### Inferences
- Narrate "Julio Menditeguy", the majority attribution (FIP, La Nación, LTA, Viviana), and note that Mar del Plata's paper credits his brother Charly. Both brothers ran the same family stud farm, which may explain the mix-up.
- The two route stories (Acapulco visit vs. Marbella membership) are not exclusive. Viviana's quote says he took the measurements from Acapulco; FIP says he was a Marbella member. An honest line: "Argentine accounts credit Julio Menditeguy, who knew both Corcuera and Hohenlohe's club."
- "Early 1970s" (La Nación) and "1975" (LTA) disagree slightly. If 1975 is right, Argentina would come only a year after Marbella's 1974 courts. Prefer "in the mid-1970s" unless a primary source surfaces.
- La Capital's "first World Championship 1987" is likely an Argentine or local event, not an FIP world championship, since FIP did not exist until 1991. This is unverified.

### Gaps
- No contemporary 1970s Argentine press source was found. Clarín, Olé and TyC Sports were blocked.
- Julio Menditeguy's role is not confirmed by an independent biography; only Find a Grave was found.
- No documented Uruguayan court before 1983 was found. The 1970s claim on Spanish Wikipedia ("spread to Uruguay…") is undated.

## 5. Early rules, first rackets and balls, and first tournaments to about 1980

### Takeaway
The first equipment was improvised. Accounts mention cut-down tennis rackets or thin wooden paddles "with little holes", later US paddle-tennis wooden paddles, and ordinary tennis balls. Rules were house rules: each Acapulco house set its own, and courts varied from 18 to 20 m. They were later written down as "Paddle Corcuera" / "the Corcuera Rules". Sources disagree on whether Enrique or Viviana wrote them and when (1969, the 1980s, 1985). No reputably sourced tournament before 1980 was found anywhere.

### Cited Findings
**Rackets and balls**
- Early play used cut-off tennis racquets and tennis balls; later they got wooden racquets used for paddle tennis in the US. — [Minter Dial (2021)](https://www.minterdial.com/2021/06/padel-tennis-courts-world/) [SPECIALIST]
- Viviana describes the first rackets as "a very, very thin wooden racket… with little holes". — [Minter Dial (2025)](https://www.minterdial.com/2025/05/rackets-walls-and-passion-viviana-corcuera-reveals-the-origins-of-padel-audio-in-spanish/) [FAMILY]
- Corcuera Jr. says the hacienda frontón game used tennis rackets and tennis balls. — [Minter Dial (2024)](https://www.minterdial.com/2024/02/enrique-corcuera/) [FAMILY]
- The Marbella Club / Foulkes account says Corcuera's game was played "with wooden paddles and tennis balls". — [Marbella Club trivia](https://www.marbellaclub.com/blog/marbella-club-anniversary-trivia) [OFFICIAL]
- Spanish blogs say early palas were all wood and forearm injuries were common. They also say early concrete courts injured players; Búsqueda says the same for Uruguay. — [PadelSpain, "Evolución de las palas"](https://www.padelspain.net/material/10549/Evolucion-de-las-palas-de-padel-de-la-madera-al-carbono/); [padelstar historic photos](https://padelstar.es/historia-del-padel/primeras-palas-de-padel-fotografias/) [WEAK]; [Búsqueda](https://www.busqueda.com.uy/tiempo-libre/el-ave-fenix-los-deportes-el-padel-regreso-y-mas-fuerza-que-nunca-n5393642) [PRESS]

**Rules**
- Corcuera published a pamphlet called "PADDLE CORCUERA" defining the court size and rules "still more or less in effect today". — [Minter Dial (2021)](https://www.minterdial.com/2021/06/padel-tennis-courts-world/) [SPECIALIST]
- The Marbella Club calls them "The Corcuera Rules". — [Marbella Club trivia](https://www.marbellaclub.com/blog/marbella-club-anniversary-trivia) [OFFICIAL]
- Spanish Wikipedia credits the first official rules to Corcuera himself. — [Wikipedia (es), Enrique Corcuera García y Pimentel](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrique_Corcuera_Garc%C3%ADa_y_Pimentel) [WIKI]
- Viviana says she wrote a booklet as a birthday present for Enrique. Padel Magazine reports this as 1969, partly disputed by the son. — [Padel Magazine](https://padel-magazine.co.uk/viviana-corcuera-hubby-is-the-inventor-of-padel/)
- Other datings of Viviana's rules: La Nación says she recalls writing them in the 1980s; El Tiempo MX says a notebook in 1985. — [La Nación (2026)](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/lifestyle/enrique-y-viviana-corcuera-el-magnate-mexicano-que-invento-el-padel-y-la-miss-argentina-que-escribio-nid01102026/); [El Tiempo MX](https://eltiempomx.com/noticia/2022/padel-el-deporte-que-surgio-en-acapulco-gracias-a-la-flojera-de-enrique-corcuera.html)
- Nallé Grinda, who played in Acapulco as a child, says "each house had its rules". Court length varied between 18 and 20 m, and some courts used the walls of the house. On one court a smash that left the court was penalised because the neighbours got so many balls.
- Jean-Noël Grinda cites house rules such as banning serve-and-volley. Viviana describes a court where hitting the air-conditioning unit on one wall meant replaying with two balls.
- Source for the two points above: [Padel Magazine, "Nallé Grinda: 'Each house had its rules in Acapulco!'"](https://padel-magazine.co.uk/nalle-grinda-every-house-had-its-rules-in-acapulco/); [Padel Magazine, Jean-Noël Grinda](https://padel-magazine.co.uk/jean-noel-grinda-on-a-toujours-vecu-padel/) [SPECIALIST]

**Tournaments**
- Santana is said to have organised padel tournaments on the Costa del Sol, but no event, venue or year is given. — [Betway blog](https://blog.betway.es/padel/padel-en-espana-una-historia-de-amor-a-primera-vista/) [WEAK]
- La Capital mentions "the first Argentine tournament run entirely by our Association, with 500 pairs entered", based at the Hotel Provincial (Mar del Plata), but gives no date. The association was founded in 1988, so this is after 1980. — [La Capital MdP](https://www.lacapitalmdp.com/historia-de-la-evolucion-del-paddle-en-argentina/amp/) [PRESS]
- Platform tennis, for comparison: the first open tournament was in Scarsdale in 1932. Paddle tennis's first tournament was in 1922. — [Platform Tennis Hall of Fame](https://platformtennishalloffame.org/history-of-the-game/); [Wikipedia (en), Paddle tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddle_tennis)

### Inferences
- Rules before 1980 were informal, varied from house to house, and were a social pastime among wealthy friends. Organised competition with associations, standard courts and national tournaments is a 1980s phenomenon: Argentina's APA in 1988, Spain's association around 1991 per FIP, FIP in 1991, a unified name and rules around 1997. The film should not invent a "first tournament" before 1980.
- "Tennis balls" is consistent across every account. Nothing found says whether they were pressurised or depressurised, or of any special type.

### Gaps
- No surviving copy or photo of the "Paddle Corcuera" rules was found.
- No pre-1980 tournament, in Mexico, Spain or Argentina, was found with a name, date and winner.
- No specific racket maker or model before 1980 was found for padel (as opposed to US paddle tennis).

## 6. Common myths and errors about padel's origins, and what better sources say

### Takeaway
The most-repeated errors are:

- a single tidy date (1969) presented as uncontested
- a "squash court" origin
- a 19th-century British ship-deck origin, or "padder tennis" on ships
- platform tennis in "the 1910s"
- Charly versus Julio Menditeguy
- confusion over whose wife Viviana was
- the idea that Corcuera's original court, or Marbella's, still stands
- a "first court in Spain in 1970"

Each is laid out below with the better-supported version.

### Cited Findings
- **"Invented in 1969", stated as settled.** Most federations and newspapers give 1969 ([FIP](https://www.padelfip.com/history/); [La Nación](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/deportes/el-extrano-invento-de-un-millonario-nid465796/); [Spanish Wikipedia biography](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrique_Corcuera_Garc%C3%ADa_y_Pimentel)). But:
  - Viviana says 1968 ([Minter Dial 2025](https://www.minterdial.com/2025/05/rackets-walls-and-passion-viviana-corcuera-reveals-the-origins-of-padel-audio-in-spanish/)).
  - English Wikipedia's infobox says first played 1968 ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padel)).
  - A "legend" and Spanish Wikipedia's "Pádel" give 1962 ([La Nación 2022](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/sabado/fue-boom-en-los-90-la-segunda-vida-del-deporte-que-marco-una-epoca-nid21012022/); [es.wiki Pádel](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A1del)).
  - The son traces it to the mid/late-1950s at Estipac ([Minter Dial 2024](https://www.minterdial.com/2024/02/enrique-corcuera/); [Padel Magazine](https://padel-magazine.co.uk/The-program-%22Watch-your-step%22-was-mistaken-about-the-origin-of-the-padel--we-checked/)).
  - Best supported: 1969 for the Las Brisas court, with earlier frontón experiments in Jalisco.
- **"He modified his squash court."** Only English Wikipedia says this ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padel)). Every other account says frontón, garden or open lot, or a planned tennis court ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A1del); [Minter Dial 2024](https://www.minterdial.com/2024/02/enrique-corcuera/); [La Nación 2022](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/sabado/fue-boom-en-los-90-la-segunda-vida-del-deporte-que-marco-una-epoca-nid21012022/); [FIP](https://www.padelfip.com/2024/03/focus-on-mexico-where-it-all-began/)). Treat "squash court" as an error.
- **"First played on 19th-century British cruise ships" / "padder tennis on ship decks".**
  - These are repeated by [LTA Padel](https://www.ltapadel.org.uk/what-we-do/what-is-the-history-of-padel/), [Red Bull](https://www.redbull.com/gb-en/history-of-padel) and others, with no primary evidence found.
  - "Padder tennis" is documented as a 1950s British and New Zealand name for a paddle-tennis variant ([British Pathé, 1951](https://www.britishpathe.com/asset/82961/); [Padder Tennis NZ](https://www.paddertennis.nz/)).
  - The well-documented deck game is deck tennis, and the Hall of Fame says the first Scarsdale platform was built for deck tennis and badminton ([Hall of Fame](https://platformtennishalloffame.org/history-of-the-game/)).
- **"Platform tennis was played in Washington and New York in the 1910s."** Platform tennis dates from 1928 in Scarsdale ([Hall of Fame](https://platformtennishalloffame.org/history-of-the-game/); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/sports/platform-tennis)). The 1910s fact is paddle tennis: Washington Square Park courts in 1915 ([en.wiki Paddle tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddle_tennis)). The two games are being conflated.
- **"Paddle tennis was invented in New York in 1921."** Beal's game dates to 1898 in Albion, Michigan; he moved to New York in 1921, and the first tournament was in 1922 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Frank-P-Beal); [en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddle_tennis); [NC POP Tennis](http://ncpoptennis.com/history-of-pop-tennis)).
- **"Hohenlohe built Spain's first courts in 1970."** This comes from weak sources ([iPadel](https://ipadel.co.uk/History-of-Padel-Tennis); [padelstar](http://padelstar.es/historia-del-padel/historia-del-padel/)). The hotel's own history (Foulkes) and both Wikipedias say 1974 ([Marbella Club trivia](https://www.marbellaclub.com/blog/marbella-club-anniversary-trivia)).
- **"The first court in Europe still stands at the Marbella Club."** The hotel's trivia says the original courts vanished in 1995, leaving a bronze plaque on a rock. Its current FAQ and Finca Ana María page speak of the first court as a landmark or "revived" ([trivia](https://www.marbellaclub.com/blog/marbella-club-anniversary-trivia); [FAQ](https://www.marbellaclub.com/faq); [Finca Ana María](https://www.marbellaclub.com/finca-ana-maria)). Best reading: a recreation or revival, not the 1974 structure.
- **"Corcuera's original court survives" / "the oldest court is in Mexico City" / "the oldest court is from 1976 at the Naranja house in Acapulco".**
  - The Corcuera court was demolished after his death in 1999 for a swimming pool ([Padel Magazine](https://padel-magazine.co.uk/viviana-corcuera-hubby-is-the-inventor-of-padel/); [Minter Dial 2025](https://www.minterdial.com/2025/05/rackets-walls-and-passion-viviana-corcuera-reveals-the-origins-of-padel-audio-in-spanish/)).
  - Ignacio Soto Borja's court in Coyoacán is described as Mexico's "first official court", inaugurated by Corcuera in **1992** ([Beyond Line](https://beyondline.com.mx/numero-5/ignacio-soto-borja-y-anda-padel-deporte-mexicano/); [ignaciosotoborjayanda.mx](https://ignaciosotoborjayanda.mx/)). It is not from the 1960s.
  - The "1976, Naranja family house" claim appears only on retailer and club blogs ([canchasdepadel.com](https://canchasdepadel.com/los-origenes-del-padel-y-la-revolucion-de-la-construccion-de-canchas-en-mexico/); [thepadellab.mx](https://thepadellab.mx/blogs/blog-del-padel/historia-del-padel)) [WEAK].
- **Viviana as "Hohenlohe's wife".** One search summary of a club blog presented "Viviana Dellavedova" as Hohenlohe's wife ([Vallparc](https://www.vallparc.com/la-amistad-hispano-mexicana-que-marco-el-origen-del-padel-en-espana/), unverified whether the error is the blog's or the summary's). Viviana Dellavedova was Enrique Corcuera's wife ([El Correo de Firmat](https://elcorreodigital.com.ar/a-62-anos-del-dia-en-que-una-firmatense-fue-elegida-miss-argentina-y-termino-marcando-la-historia-del-padel/); [La Nación 2026](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/lifestyle/enrique-y-viviana-corcuera-el-magnate-mexicano-que-invento-el-padel-y-la-miss-argentina-que-escribio-nid01102026/)). Hohenlohe's first wife was Ira von Fürstenberg ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ira_von_F%C3%BCrstenberg)).
- **"Viviana wrote the rules in 1969" vs. "Corcuera wrote them".** This is unresolved. It is Viviana's testimony (1969 birthday booklet / "the 80s" / 1985) against Spanish Wikipedia and Minter Dial crediting Enrique's "Paddle Corcuera" pamphlet, with the son partly disputing her account ([Padel Magazine fact-check](https://padel-magazine.co.uk/The-program-%22Watch-your-step%22-was-mistaken-about-the-origin-of-the-padel--we-checked/)).
- **"Charly Menditeguy brought padel to Argentina."** This is a minority attribution ([La Capital MdP](https://www.lacapitalmdp.com/historia-de-la-evolucion-del-paddle-en-argentina/)). FIP, La Nación and Viviana name Julio ([FIP](https://www.padelfip.com/history/); [La Nación](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/lifestyle/enrique-y-viviana-corcuera-el-magnate-mexicano-que-invento-el-padel-y-la-miss-argentina-que-escribio-nid01102026/)).
- **"Padel" as a backronym.** The P.A.D.E.L. acronym ("Promoción de Actividades Deportivas, Educativas y Lúdicas") was an administrative device for registering a Spanish association ([Echegaray](https://es.linkedin.com/pulse/p%C3%A1del-se-escribe-con-e%C3%B1e-de-momento-mart%C3%ADn-echegaray-vales); [FIP es](https://www.padelfip.com/es/2024/10/de-argentina-y-espana-a-la-fama-mundial-el-fenomeno-del-padel-que-conquisto-el-mundo/)). The RAE derives the word from English "paddle (tennis)" ([DLE](https://dle.rae.es/p%C3%A1del)).

## 7. Visual reference for the illustrator: courts, materials, settings, clothing, era

### Takeaway
There is enough sourced detail to paint the two key courts.

- **Acapulco, 1969:** a firm-surfaced court of about 20 × 10 m on a sloping, densely vegetated hillside lot in Las Brisas. One end abuts the wall of the house; there are 3 m walls at the ends, metal mesh or fencing, and a tennis net in the middle.
- **Marbella, 1974:** the Marbella Club's existing tennis court, net uprooted, cement walls at either end, chicken-wire-style mesh along the sides. Today it is marked by a bronze plaque set in a large rock.

Clothing and people's appearance are **not** documented in anything found; that is a gap the illustrator must not fill with confident detail.

### Inferences
- A defensible Acapulco painting: a white- or grey-rendered concrete end wall about 3 m high, part of it the house's own wall, with wire mesh above or along the sides. A hard surface and a tennis net across the middle, steep green tropical slope and bay beyond. Two pairs with small wooden bats and yellow or white tennis balls. Do not paint glass walls, artificial turf or blue courts: glass and turf came later ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padel)).
- Ball colour: tennis balls in 1969 could be white or yellow. Yellow balls were introduced to tennis in the early 1970s (general knowledge, not sourced here; verify before relying on it).
