A 30-second webinar promo: the film a host posts so people register.

First find the webinar: a real, free, public webinar that takes place between October 12 and November 30, 2026, from a well-known organisation outside the tech industry (for example the National Association of REALTORS, the American Heart Association, the American Nurses Association, the World Health Organization, or a university's public health or business school). It must have its own registration page with a title, a date and time with a time zone, and at least one named speaker, ideally with a photo. Never choose one featuring a Russian speaker or organisation. Use that page's facts only.

Beats:
1. A "LIVE WEBINAR" tag and the date, in the host's colours, with its logo.
2. The title set large, arriving phrase by phrase, fitted however long it is.
3. The speaker or speakers (one to three): each one's photo from the page in a clean frame, with name, role and organisation. With no photo, the name stands alone in type.
4. "You'll learn": up to three points, only from the page's own description. With none published, hold the title longer instead.
5. The date, the time with its time zone, the length, and "Free" or the credit offered (CE, CPE) only if the page says so.
6. The end card: "Register" with the link, and the host's logo.

How to make it (this film will become a template others remake for their own event):
- Use only facts published on the webinar's page or on the host's own pages. Never invent a date, time, name or number; a fact you cannot find is left out and the layout still holds.
- Use the host's real logo and its own colours and type (fetch them from its site). Draw nothing generic where the real thing exists. No mascots, no jokes, no theme costume: clean, brand-led, typographic motion design of the kind a good in-house designer would post.
- Keep every fact, colour and picture name in one `const FACTS = {...}` object at the top of film.js, and nothing of them in the code below it.
- Fit long values (shrink, then wrap); lists lay out for 1 to 4 items; an optional item that is missing leaves no hole.
- Lay everything out from the frame's width and height, not fixed pixels, so the same code holds in 16:9, 1:1 and 9:16.
- Narration: short, warm, plain. Say the day and time the way a person would. Never read a URL aloud.
- Readable on a phone: nothing important smaller than a caption.
