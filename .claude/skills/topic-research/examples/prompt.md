Anthropic wants to go public at up to $2 trillion. Its draft filing shows $16.3 billion of revenue in six months, an $80.7 billion loss (mostly not cash) and over $600 billion promised for computers. Can it ever earn that price? A research film with critical thinking that takes its time to explain: the evidence on screen, every idea explained for a newcomer, both sides, and what the data does not show.

A research film of about 13 minutes, 16:9 landscape (not vertical), in this project's look: the red strap and lower third, real pages, paper. It is made the way this project's Yandex bulletin was made, with these changes, all asked for by the channel's owner:

1. THE WORDS. The narration is attached word for word: doc3, lines L01 to L72. In vo.json use those lines exactly, in that order, one vo line per L-line, nothing added, nothing reworded, and NONE LEFT OUT: the pilot of this film dropped a line (the valuation history), so before rendering, count vo.json's lines and check there are 72, L01 to L72. doc3 also says what is on screen during each line: follow it.

2. THE VOICE. This overrides the project's voice line. In vo.json use the voice "Orus" with this style, word for word: "Confident and engaged, like a reporter walking a viewer through evidence: clear, firm emphasis on the key numbers, a steady voice, letting each number land. Not flat, not shouting, not alarmed". Never the voice "Charon". There is no rush: a short pause after each key number and a breath between sections. No music at all; only quiet paper sounds on the cuts.

3. RESEARCH, NOT BREAKING NEWS. The strap label is "RESEARCH" and, at its right, "DATA AS OF OCT 21, 2026". The film's headline sits on its own band under the strap through the opening: "Anthropic wants $2 trillion. Can it earn it?" A section label at the top right changes at each heading of doc3 ("0 · THE BASICS", "1 · THE PRICE" ... "11 · NOT IN THE DATA"). Every number the film sets large carries a small tag with its kind, from doc1: FILED, MEASURED, REPORTED, ESTIMATE or CLAIM, and a source line under it.

4. EXPLAIN, DON'T ASSUME. The viewer may know nothing about finance or AI. Where doc3 says EXPLAINER, the film draws its own simple explanation in its type: a short definition and a small diagram whose arrows and parts draw on as the words are said (an IPO, an S-1, a valuation, a run-rate as a speedometer, price-to-sales, a convertible note, GAAP vs adjusted, compute, a gigawatt in homes, the grid's waiting line, tokens, API vs subscription, gross margin, risk factors, fair use, voting control, a TAM, who keeps the value of an invention). Plain shapes, arrows, dots and type only; never a drawn person, building, server, chip, robot, money or logo.

5. NOTHING STATIC. A long film must never look like a slideshow. From frame 0 something is always moving:
- every real page and board sits under a slow push in or pull out with a slight sideways drift for as long as it is on screen; when the narrator reads a line on a page, the camera pushes onto that line, underlines it as it is said, and eases back after;
- every chart is built on screen as the narration reaches it (doc2 gives the data and how each moves): bars grow from zero one after another, lines draw left to right, figures count up to their value as they are said, a highlighted bar or label arrives on its word;
- pages and cards slide in and settle; they do not slam, flash or bounce;
- no hold longer than about two seconds without motion; change the picture or the framing at least every four seconds.
Use the kit (SK.bars, SK.chart, SK.counter, SK.stat, SK.cues tied to words) for charts and cards; cues hang off the narration's words.

6. READABLE, ALWAYS (what the pilot got wrong). A one-card picture (upload4, the ranking; upload5, the 10-Q passage) fills the frame: at least 90% of the frame's width, never small in the middle of empty paper. On a board, zoom so the card being read fills most of the frame. When the camera pushes onto text, the whole headline or quoted line stays inside the frame; never cut a line of text at the frame's edge while it is on screen. In the opening pile, each new page lands lower and to the right so the headline under it stays readable. In the L04 montage each picture fills the frame, zoomed into one readable part (doc3 says which), never a small thumbnail.

7. THE OPENING HOLDS THE VIEWER (L01-L05, about 25 seconds). At 0.0 s the strap, the headline band and upload1 are already on screen, and the frame is already moving. upload2 slides in at about 2 s, upload3 at about 4 s, each named in the lower third ("Yahoo Finance · Oct 21, 2026", "TNW, reporting the Wall Street Journal · Oct 21, 2026", "Polymarket on X · Oct 20, 2026"). The film's headline is never placed over a page's own headline. L03 is the hook: three large numbers side by side ($16.3B, $80.7B, $600B+) counting up; on "not what it looks like" the $80.7B card tilts and gets a small "MOSTLY NOT CASH" tag; on "bigger than it looks" the $600B+ card grows. L04 is the montage of what is coming, ending on the card "What would have to be true?" with the chapter list growing under it. Never open on a title card, a logo or a scene-setter.

PURE FACTS. doc1 (the fact sheet) is the only source of facts and doc2 (the chart data) the only source of chart values: use their exact numbers, names and dates; read doc1's "Unconfirmed / not known", "Commonly misreported" and "The film must not say" before writing a line. This project's brief forbids invented charts: for this film, the charts in doc2 are allowed and required, drawn from doc2's values only, each with its source line; no other chart, figure or number may be drawn.
