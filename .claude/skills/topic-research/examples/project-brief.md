A research film: one question people are already searching for, answered from primary data with critical thinking. Each film takes its time to explain, shows its evidence on screen, gives both sides, and ends on what the data does not show. 16:9 landscape, 8 to 15 minutes, watched on a laptop or a phone by someone who typed the subject's name into YouTube and may know nothing about the field.

Each film comes with three documents and its sources:
- doc1, the fact sheet: the only source of facts, every one labelled FILED, MEASURED, REPORTED, ESTIMATE or CLAIM, with "Unconfirmed", "Commonly misreported" and "The film must not say". Read all of it before writing a line.
- doc2, the chart data: the only source of chart values, each chart with its source line and how it moves.
- doc3, the running order and the narration, word for word: lines L01, L02 ... each with what is on screen while it is said. vo.json says every line exactly, in order, one vo line each (or several in a row for one over the voice's limit), nothing added, reworded or left out. The film is as long as these lines take to say.
- The sources: attached pictures, and pages the film photographs itself with the page tool (keep: the masthead down to the headline, or the paragraph that is read; posts on X through their embed page). Every page is shown exactly as it is: never redrawn, retyped, recoloured or cropped through its text.

THE LOOK. The research desk on paper: a red strap across the top reading "RESEARCH" with "DATA AS OF <the date the prompt gives>" at its right; a lower-third band naming the outlet and date of the page on screen (for a chart, its source line; for an explainer, "Explainer"); a section label at the top right that changes at each heading of doc3 ("1 · THE PRICE"). Heavy plain type, the story's own accent, never green. No music: only quiet paper sounds on the cuts.

THE OPENING HOLDS THE VIEWER (about the first 25 seconds). At 0.0 s the strap, the film's question on a headline band under it, and the main source's page are already on screen, and the frame is already moving. More pages slide in, each landing lower and to the right so the headline under it stays readable, each named in the lower third. Then the hook: the two or three numbers the question turns on, set large and counting up, one of them "not what it looks like". Then a montage of what is coming, each picture filling the frame, zoomed into one readable part, ending on the question with the chapter list growing under it. Never open on a title card, a logo or a scene-setter.

EXPLAIN, DON'T ASSUME. Wherever doc3 says EXPLAINER, the film draws its own simple explanation in its type: a short definition and a small diagram whose arrows and parts draw on as the words are said. Plain shapes, arrows, dots and type only: never a drawn person, building, server, chip, robot, money or logo.

NOTHING STATIC. A long film must never look like a slideshow.
- Every real page sits under a slow camera for as long as it is on screen (SK.read). When the narrator reads a line on a page, the camera pushes onto that line, marks it as it is said, and eases back.
- Every chart is built on screen as the narration reaches it: bars grow from zero one after another, lines draw left to right, figures count up as they are said, a highlight arrives on its word (SK.bars, SK.chart, SK.counter, SK.stat, cues on the narration's words).
- Pages and cards slide in and settle; they never slam, flash or bounce.
- No hold longer than about two seconds without motion; a new picture or a new framing at least every four seconds.

READABLE, ALWAYS. A page that is read fills most of the frame's width: a one-card picture at least 90%. When the camera pushes onto text, the whole headline or quoted line stays inside the frame: never a line cut at the frame's edge while it is on screen, never a page small in the middle of empty paper, never a montage of thumbnails. After the opening, one page at a time.

EVERY NUMBER CARRIES ITS KIND. Every number the film sets large has a small tag from doc1 (FILED, MEASURED, REPORTED, ESTIMATE, CLAIM) and a source line under it. Charts come only from doc2's values, each with its source line; no other chart, figure or number is drawn.

BOTH SIDES, THEN WHAT IS NOT KNOWN. What the people who disagree say is shown as their own posts, real screenshots with their names. Near the end a section on what the data does not show. The last line is the open question; then stop: no summary, no sign-off, no call to action, no web address. The last two seconds: a small "Made with KitCut" with the KitCut logo (pic_kitcut_logo) over the held frame.

THE VOICE. In vo.json use the voice "Orus" with this style, word for word: "Confident and engaged, like a reporter walking a viewer through evidence: clear, firm emphasis on the key numbers, a steady voice, letting each number land. Not flat, not shouting, not alarmed". Never the voice "Charon". A short pause after each key number and a breath between sections. Say acronyms and names as doc3's pronunciations say.

FACTS. Exact numbers, names, dates and spellings from doc1; each source's own caution ("says", "reports", "estimates") stays attached to its claim. No adjectives about people. No hype words, no exclamation marks. No party politics and no politicians unless doc3 names them. Nothing Russian is shown as neutral or admirable: no Russian people, brands, symbols or imagery unless the film is about them, and then as doc3 tells it. Never an invented person, quote, chart or screenshot, and never a real person's face unless their own photo or post is among the sources. Captions are added by the studio: never your own. Keep every moving thing out of the caption band at the bottom of the frame.
